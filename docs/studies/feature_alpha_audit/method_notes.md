# UFC-method audit notes

Research audit, 2 October 2026. These notes review the existing study; they do not approve a model or roadmap. Original files were read without modification. The accompanying chronology experiment writes only to this audit's folders.

## What deserves further work

### 1. Men's short-memory individual-statistic profile

**Observed in the original study:** the 16-input decayed adjperf/adjdef profile gains 0.51 milli-log-loss over the average bookmaker baseline (ordinary per-match interval −0.05 to 1.07), and 0.92 in 2022–2026. It is scored on 31,661 men's matches with odds. The women's gain is 0.02 (−0.51 to 0.54). See [Stage D](../ufc_ideas/stageD.md), the profile table and its season/level breakdown, and `data/studies/ufc_ideas/stageD_profile_results.csv`.

**Why retain it:** this is the strongest direct market-conditioned lead in the UFC study, and its signs are consistently positive from 2020 onward. The effect remains small relative to uncertainty. An especially favorable level or era is a hypothesis generated by these data, not an independently validated segment.

**Problems with its interpretation:** being weak alone does not establish that a feature is independent of the market. Stage D's statement that low standalone predictability is “what makes it independent of a win rating” is too strong. Conditional out-of-sample performance is the relevant evidence. Nor does beating shuffled multi-input models establish that the profile beats the market: the original study correctly notes that unpenalized noise models lose by fitting many unnecessary coefficients. The ordinary market improvement interval still crosses zero.

**Targeted retests:** retain the prespecified profile, use training-only scaling and regularization selected from preceding seasons, evaluate on identical rows against both average and Pinnacle prices, and quantify uncertainty in event/week clusters. Distinguish raw-market comparisons from fitted market-calibration comparisons. Use grouped ablations (serve style, break/game statistics, unmatched adjdef) to understand the signal without choosing a winning statistic from the evaluation period. The separate `profile_retest.py` audit implements the first group of checks.

**What may have been left untested:** every statistic borrows constants from serve points, return points, or total points, through `stageB_adjperf.constants_for` and `skill_of`. The two-week memory was optimized for gain over Elo, not gain over the market. A small, grouped, training-only comparison of recent innovations against longer-run level is justified if the existing profile survives sharper-price and chronology checks. A large renewed search over individually tuned statistics is not yet warranted.

Source: `src/eda/ufc_ideas/stageD_profile.py:42` defines the statistics; `:84` defines the 16-input profile; `stageB_adjperf.py:102` maps statistics to the three core families; `:361` reads the frozen constants.

### 2. Recent opponent-adjusted point performance beyond Elo

**Observed:** two-week total-points adjperf gains 3.19 milli-log-loss over Elo for men and 3.32 for women. Beyond a model that refits Elo and the existing serve/return rating together, it still adds 0.70 and 1.48. Serve-points adjperf adds 0.83 and 1.43 beyond that same combined baseline. This is stronger evidence of useful additional prediction information than its almost-null performance against the closing line. See [Stage B](../ufc_ideas/stageB.md), “Does anything add to the ratings through the iid model?”

**Implementation appears coherent under the stated time convention:** opponent means/MADs are read before the historical match; the resulting historical performance is then averaged only before the forecast match. Adjdef mirrors are deliberately deduplicated. The implemented identity for paired serve/return statistics is correct and independently tested in the original study. Keeping all mirrored copies would add no information.

**What needs disentangling:** the feature is `sum(weight × z)/(sum(weight) + K)`. At a two-week half-life, K=10 or 20, and especially K=160 for return performance, this can behave more like a recent sum than a stable mean. Amount played and recent progress through a draw can therefore affect the feature even when underlying quality is similar. This is a interpretation question, not by itself leakage.

The original study already did a useful partial check: removing same-event rounds reduces total-points gains from 3.19 to 2.09 for men and 3.32 to 1.83 for women. For men, its gain beyond Elo plus serve/return ratings drops from 0.70 to 0.17. Thus the current event matters materially; the signal is not entirely confined to that event.

**Targeted next test:** separate prior-event recent form, current-event performance, and observed recent match/round load. Compare their additions to Elo plus serve/return strength on the same observations. A two-part form estimate (estimated quality plus effective sample/recency) makes this distinction clearer than assuming every improvement is “hot form.” Differences across current versus previous surface are a plausible narrow extension; the current features center observations on population surface means but pool a player's history across surfaces.

Source: `src/eda/ufc_ideas/stageB_adjperf.py:217` implements step-three sums; `:228` computes the shrunk player values; `stageB_split.py:25` documents the earlier-weeks-only diagnostic; `stageB_common.py:153` transforms and surface-centers observations.

### 3. Short-memory IOS, primarily for prediction beyond Elo

**Observed:** the short-memory serve-points IOS matchup gains 3.72 milli-log-loss over Elo for men (four weeks) and 5.02 for women (13 weeks), compared with 1.08 and 2.21 for the IOS settings chosen to predict the next statistic. Gains beyond Elo plus existing serve/return ratings are 0.50 and 0.64. Against the average closing line the gains are 0.13 and 0.01. See [Stage B](../ufc_ideas/stageB.md), tuning, diagnostic, and combined-baseline tables.

**The objective mismatch matters:** the original IOS grid minimizes next-statistic loss per point. The residual feature task asks what adds to an existing match-win forecast. Those are different objectives, so the short-memory improvement gives a reason to retest a small recent-versus-long-run model on the residual objective. It does not justify declaring the original long-memory statistical estimator defective.

**Selection limitation:** the four-week/13-week diagnostic rows were introduced after the first all-season results had been read, then the best one for each tour was included in Stage D. The additional rows were not numerically optimized on the test labels, but the choice to introduce and retain them was informed by those results. Calling 2019–2026 entirely clean for this branch overstates its status.

**Two concrete technical obstacles:**

* `stageB_ios.matchup_points` calls `B.F.best_of_five(rows)` with its default `source="score"` (`stageB_ios.py:430`, `stage4_features.py:194`). That helper reads the match's eventual sets won as well as the supplied best-of field. Its `format` input is also inherited from the match table. The conversion from ratings to pre-match probability needs scheduled format/rules, not the realized score. This affects the promising aggregate IOS matchup, unlike the pure historical adjperf profile.
* The joint IOS solver releases every observation whose tournament **start date** precedes the snapshot (`stageB_ios.py:224`). Results from late rounds of an overlapping fortnight event can therefore influence another event's ratings before they occurred, through common opponents. The original [Stage B](../ufc_ideas/stageB.md), “Problems found,” explicitly recognizes this but calls it small without measuring the effect. Existing probes validate this start-date convention rather than actual result availability.

**Potential missed branch:** the individual-statistic IOS profiles use long memories (mostly 26–208 weeks); the short-memory experiment was only for the serve-points aggregate. Consequently, the null long-memory IOS profile does not test a recent IOS innovation profile. It is a secondary research candidate after repairing format and availability, not current evidence of market alpha.

## Opponent adjustment: retain the idea, narrow the revision

The one-step adjperf adjustment does not fully eliminate strength of opposition: within player-seasons, adjusted total-points performance still falls by 0.19 and 0.17 standardization units per 100 opponent Elo points (men/women). The opponent's “allowed” mean also depends on whom that opponent faced. This is compatible with incomplete adjustment or nonlinear matchup effects; it is not proof of a player-specific strong-versus-weak skill.

The original study directly tested the latter: the gap's year-to-year correlations are only 0.08 and 0.09, and split/slope/interaction models lose or add very little. Do not reopen those broad trait searches. A more focused comparison would use a historical statistic residual against a joint, pre-match IOS expectation, then estimate recent deviations with an appropriate uncertainty measure. The current MAD standardizes opponent variability, not the uncertainty of the current match's count. Compare simpler unstandardized or predictive-variance residuals before increasing model complexity.

Source: [Stage B](../ufc_ideas/stageB.md), “Who the statistics were earned against”; `stageB_adjperf.py:191` constructs the MAD-standardized z; `stageB_split.py:1` documents tested opponent groups, slopes, and interactions.

## Calendar-week sensitivity experiment

`src/eda/feature_alpha_audit/chronology.py` rebuilds only the 16 adjperf-profile inputs at their original frozen constants. It maps every tournament date to its preceding/same Monday, including Sunday backwards, and preserves original event identity, outcome/year labels, model baselines, source records, and the original studies. Both original and altered profiles are compared on the intersection where all inputs exist.

This experiment changes history eligibility and decay ages together. It is therefore a sensitivity analysis, **not** a corrected pre-match dataset. It does not resolve overlapping fortnight events, establish when box scores became available, or rebuild Elo. The original unpenalized scorer is retained here only to isolate this feature change; the sharper-price and regularized audit is separate.

Three targeted tests pass: calendar mapping across a year boundary, exclusion of another midweek event while retaining same-event earlier rounds, and numerical equivalence of the restricted 16-input builder to the original builder on synthetic histories. Outputs are `chronology_calendar_week_features.parquet`, `chronology_feature_deltas.csv`, `chronology_scores.csv`, and `chronology_summary.json` under `data/studies/feature_alpha_audit/`. The retained `chronology_profile_arrays.npz` checkpoint permits rescoring without rebuilding histories.

Gains are milli-log-loss versus the original scorer's season-refitted baseline; these are not untouched validation results. Normalization moves 26,019 of 872,704 match-level event dates. The smallest per-column original-versus-normalized correlation is 0.9960 for ATP and 0.9956 for WTA. The men's market lead shrinks modestly and remains positive; women's market results remain near zero. The experiment therefore does **not** support dismissing the men's profile as an off-Monday date artifact, and does **not** establish complete chronological validity. Paired regularized/Pinnacle scoring of the saved profile is reported separately by the profile audit.



For reproducibility on this Windows Conda installation, prepend the tennis environment's Library/bin to the process PATH before invoking ./python.sh. Otherwise a first BLAS operation such as np.corrcoef can terminate the process without a Python traceback. The builder supports --build-only (persist the numeric NPZ checkpoint and exit) and --assemble-only (load that checkpoint in a fresh process and score); no model settings change between these commands.
