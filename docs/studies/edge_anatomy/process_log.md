* **How it was read** (before any result):
  * Four deliverables: (1) a brainstorm by a small agent team of what a model "built around what the recent close does not price" would look like; (2) a measurement of which parts of the current best model's information the 2025–26 closing line prices and which it does not; (3) a diagnosis of the recent (2025–26) edge in that light; (4) a plain-language `.md` (this folder's README) plus a chat summary, written for a business reader with a little statistics, data science and tennis background.
  * **"The most recent closing line" has two readings, both acted on, since they lead to the same design:**
    * (a) *the latest price before the match* (the close, the sharpest public price), as opposed to an opening or mid-way line;
    * (b) *the market as it prices today*: markets learn, so a statistic that beat the 2015 close may be priced by the 2025 close; the test must be against recent seasons' closes.
  * **"Built around that idea"** is read as a model whose structure *starts from* the close and spends its capacity only on the residual (what the close leaves unexplained), with candidate statistics admitted by their residual value against recent closes. The current model (TabPFN with the close as one of 57 inputs, trained on 2013..Y−1) is the incumbent this is compared with, conceptually and, where cheap, numerically.
  * **"Is the decade-long line in a non-linear model wrong?"** has been partly tested already (training_window, close_drift, meta_stack, market_anchoring; all read ≤ 2024 except market_anchoring's 2026). This study does not re-run those; it uses their results and adds what they lack: the anatomy of the edge on 2025–26.
  * Set aside: building and adopting a new production model in this session (the adoption route is the owner's call and needs a candidate build and `compare.py`); betting-venue questions (bet_timing, line_sharpening cover them).
  * No question was put to the owner: the session runs unattended and the readings above are compatible.
* **The target** ([adoption framework](../adoption_framework/README.md) § 1), fixed before any result was read:
  * **Price:** the model's own odds input at the close, margin removed (Pinnacle's close to 7 Sep 2025, then BetOnline's closing line, the books' average where neither prices the match), recalibrated walk-forward with one slope per tour (the decided recipe, `compare.close_forecast`). Secondary prices: the raw margin-free input; Pinnacle alone where it exists.
  * **Matches:** the current instance's frame (main tour, both tours), debut seasons out, as the decided comparisons.
  * **Seasons:** recent = season 2025 to 28 Jul 2026 (the "H-exposed" part of the proposed holdout); comparison era 2020–2024. **Matches from 29 Jul 2026 (the proposed sealed holdout) are not read.**
  * **Declared primary (a diagnostic, not an adoption test):** the current instance's gain over its own recalibrated price on 2025 – 28 Jul 2026, both tours, both stages, and its split across input families by the nested ladder of § 3.

**A. The edge itself.** The current instance, both stages (16-seed mean; slopes and b walk-forward on 2019..Y−1 from seeds 0–3, as `pinnacle_first/backtest.py`), against its recalibrated price: 2025–Jul 2026 and 2020–2024, by tour; plus the raw price, Pinnacle alone, and what the recalibration alone is worth.

**B. The ladder and leave-one-family-out refits** (local TabPFN-3.5, the decided config, seeds 0–3, seasons 2019–2026, written to `data/studies/edge_anatomy/fits/`):
* ladder (nested): L1 price; L2 + context; L3 + ratings; L4 + schedule and body; L5 + box-score form; L6 = the full 57 (the instance's stored fits, seeds 0–3);
* leave one family out of the full model: −context, −ratings, −schedule, −form, −IOS;
* each arm through the decided recipe (its own walk-forward per-tour slope; stage 2 for men with its own b), scored against the recalibrated price on 2025–Jul 2026 (primary) and 2020–2024; every comparison paired on matches, seeds paired.
* 10 new arms × 8 seasons × 4 seeds = 320 fits.

**C. CPU analyses** (no refit):
* C1, the residual scan: each non-price input (A − B difference for per-player pairs) and the speed column, one at a time, as a nudge on the recalibrated close (logistic with the close's log-odds as an offset), by era (2013–16, 2017–19, 2020–22, 2023–24, 2025–Jul 26): coefficient, cluster-robust z (four-week blocks), and the walk-forward out-of-sample gain; then each family as a ridge nudge. Context inputs enter as interactions with the price (a context-dependent slope), since alone they cannot move a symmetric forecast.
* C2, the disagreement's anatomy: d = the model's log-odds minus the recalibrated price's; a linear surrogate of d on the inputs by family (how much of the disagreement each family explains) and how much of each family's part came true (its realisation).
* C3, where the 2025–26 gain sits: tour, level, surface, favourite or underdog, size of disagreement, the odds input's source (Pinnacle or the BetOnline stand-in), month.
* C4, market learning: does a family's residual value fall from era to era?

**D. The agent team.** Round 1, three independent brainstormers, launched before any result of this study is read (they may read the repository's reports, which hold earlier 2025–26 results): a betting-market practitioner, a statistics/ML architect, a tennis-signal scout. Round 2, after the results: a red team attacks the diagnosis; the lead synthesises. Briefs verbatim in `briefs/`.

**Reading rules** (fixed now). Gains in mLL, **+ = the named arm or family better**; 95% intervals over four-week blocks (seeds paired where both sides have seeds). A family "adds" when the interval clears 0; "no detectable addition" when the upper end is below +0.30 mLL (the size worth betting on, about the resolution of an odds-change comparison); otherwise "unresolved" with P(better). Never "tie". Ladder steps depend on order; leave-one-out gives the unique part; both are reported.

**The lead's expectation before any result** (recorded so that hindsight cannot rewrite it): much of the edge will be a context-dependent recalibration of the close (favourite–longshot by tour and level), ratings will add about nothing given the close, box-score form and the IOS profile a modest share, stage 2 a small men's share. If that holds, the advice's answer is that the model's statistics carry less of the edge than its structure does.

## 6. Judgment calls and deviations

1. **The sealed matches.** Options: read all of 2026 (to the frame's end, early Oct) or stop at 28 Jul 2026. Chosen: stop at 28 Jul, as the backtest and the proposed holdout do, so the sealed matches stay usable for a prospective check. Before any result.
2. **`adjperf_tpw_2w` in the form family.** It is a two-week opponent-adjusted share of points won, a points-won aggregate that the owner's thesis would class near the ratings. Kept with form (short memory) because its memory is two weeks; the leave-one-out arm shows its family's unique part either way. Before any result.
3. **Analyses added after the first results** (each a deviation from § 3, flagged in the chronology): the input-source check (after the OddsTrader outlier), the 2025/2026 split of the anatomy (after the women's fade), the line-move test (after the scan; it implements the advice's reading (a)), the per-statistic ledger with BH q-values (multiplicity), the advice-process test (`advice.py`), the transparent anchored model (`residual.py`), the re-sizing layer (`layer.py`, after the architect's memo and the realisation 1.80), the same-match benchmark check (after the scout's memo), the charts. None changes the declared primary; each is reported whatever it showed (the layer and the recent selection came out negative and are reported).
4. **The anatomy's surrogate.** Options: TabPFN refits per family (the ladder, plan B, slow), a linear surrogate of the disagreement (fast, outcome-free), or SHAP-type attributions (not installed). Chosen: the linear surrogate with three price-shape terms (L, L|L|, L³) and context-by-price interactions, fitted per window. Before any anatomy result; after Part A. Its "worth" (the gain lost when a part is removed) is not additive across parts; the parts are correlated (the ratings, form and IOS parts especially), so a part's worth is its marginal value given the others. The ladder and leave-one-out refits are the check.
5. **The scan's family test.** The planned Wald test failed numerically (chronology 18:46–19:10); replaced by a cross-fitted gain, with ridge 5 and then ridge 1000. The ridge-1000 variant was added after seeing ridge 5 lose everywhere; both are reported. The lesson drawn (a single era cannot estimate many nudges) rests on ridge 5; the family readings rest on ridge 1000.
6. **Labels in the ledger** (`leads.py`): |z| ≥ 2 thresholds, written in the file before the table was read, after the scan and line-move z's had been seen. They describe; the BH q-values carry the multiplicity.
7. **The advice-process selection** used `scan_learning.csv`'s inverse-variance pooled z over 2013–24 (four era fits pooled), not one fit on 2013–24. Chosen for speed; before the advice result. Selection read no 2025–26 result.
8. **The anchored model's ridge** is chosen inside each training window from 10, 100, 1000, 10000 on the window's last season. The choice jumps between 10 and 10,000 from season to season (`residual_lambdas.csv`): a noisy tuner. The tie with TabPFN holds for 2025 (λ 10) and 2026 (λ 1000) alike.
9. **The layer's prior** (delta ~ N(0, 0.5²)) was fixed in the file before its run; no other prior was tried.
10. **The clock.** Chronology times written before 18:52 were estimates that ran ahead of the clock; corrected by added rows, the order unchanged.

## 8. Weak points

* **The inputs were chosen with 2022–2026 in view** (feature_provenance). Every 2025–26 reading of the model, its families and its statistics is therefore in-sample with respect to the choice of inputs. The advice-process test is clean in its selection rule, not in its candidate pool. The honest check is the sealed matches (from 29 Jul 2026), from about February 2027.
* **2026's benchmark is BetOnline's line**, about 0.4–0.5 mLL softer than Pinnacle's on shared matches of 2024–25. In 2026 the men's edge survives the exchanges (Betfair, Polymarket) and the model fed the exchange price; the women's does not. The exchange closes are timed differently (Betfair untimed; Polymarket 30 minutes before the listed start, with about 2% of markets showing in-play prices per sharp_benchmark), so some of the women's gap may be timing rather than sharpness.
* **Multiplicity.** About 49 inputs × 2 tours × 5 eras were scanned; BH q-values are given for 2025–26 only. The ledger's labels use |z| ≥ 2 thresholds; single-era patterns can be luck (the men's serve/return asymmetry is the most exposed).
* **The anatomy's decomposition** is a linear surrogate; its parts are correlated, so a part's worth depends on the others (§ 6.4). The ladder and leave-one-out refits address this.
* **The line-move test** uses BetOnline's opening line (a median 26 hours out; the time varies by match) and BetOnline's close, not Pinnacle's; a statistic "learned by the close" at BetOnline may be priced earlier at Pinnacle.
* **Archived closes, not fills.** No limits, delays or account restrictions.
* **Where a flaw would most likely hide:** in the women's numbers (data gaps after June 2026, per the scout; the feed switch, per the red team) and in the 2026 benchmark.
* **The ladder's order and the leave-one-out arms** are both conditional on the other families present; the family definitions (§ 3) were fixed before any result, but another grouping (e.g. `adjperf_tpw_2w` with the ratings, § 6.2) would move the shares. Four seeds per arm; the seed term is paired.
* **Several analyses were added after results were seen** (§ 6.3). Each is reported whatever it showed; the advice-process test was designed after the 2025–26 ledger had been read (F15).
* **What a re-design would do:** score everything against Pinnacle's own 2025–26 closes (paid sources exist); pre-register the ledger's labels and q-thresholds before any look; run the family ladder on the sealed matches; group the families by a pre-registered rule tested for robustness to regrouping.
* **The one experiment most likely to overturn the conclusions:** the sealed look of 2027 at the five lead bundles and the men's edge against Pinnacle's live closes. If the schedule family's unique part and the men's edge both read near 0 there, the 2020–26 numbers were the selection's luck.
