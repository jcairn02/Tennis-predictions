How beatable is Polymarket tennis before the match?

We looked at every bet placed before the start of 27,968 tennis matches on Polymarket, $574.4M in all, and asked one question of each account: does it consistently get a better price than the last price before the match? That last price turns out to be accurate, so an account that beats it has a real edge.

Polymarket tennis can be beaten before the match, but narrowly and by few accounts. About 1 in 23 regular pre-match accounts clearly gets better prices than the closing price, and as a group they beat it by 1.3¢ per $1 bet, 0.9¢ after Polymarket's fees. About 1 in 7 clearly gets worse prices. Everyone else can't be told apart from luck, and on average gives up a little.

By level of play, the winners' edge is similar everywhere, 1–2¢ per $1 before fees. It is highest at Challenger and WTA 125 events (1.8¢ before fees, 1.3¢ after) and lowest, and least certain, at the Grand Slams (0.7¢). What changes a lot is the size of the pool: $201.7M was bet before Grand Slam matches, $75.7M before Challenger and WTA 125 matches, and $7.3M before ITF matches, where the clear winners supply 32% of the money.

A
The yardstick

What "beating the closing price" means

The closing price is the last price before the match starts. If you bought a player at 50¢ and the price closed at 52¢, you beat the close by 2¢ a share. Per dollar bet that is 4¢, because each share cost 50¢. This is what bettors call closing-line value.

In dollars. You bet $100 on a player at 50¢ and get 200 shares. The price closes at 50.75¢. If the closing price is the true chance of winning, those 200 shares are worth $101.50 on average. You are $1.50 ahead, or 1.5¢ per $1, which is about what the clear winners manage. In betting odds, it is like taking +100 when the fair price is about −103.

Fees. Polymarket charges a fee when you accept a price someone else has posted. It is 5% of (1 − the price) of the amount you bet: 2.5¢ per $1 at 50¢, 1¢ at 80¢. Posting your own price is free. That fee is why the "after fees" figures on this page are smaller.

Beating the closing price only means something if the closing price is right. It is: across every settled match, favourites won about as often as their closing price said.

Is the closing price the real chance of winning?

Every settled match, by the favourite's price at the close

Strong favourites (80¢ and up) won 1.5 and 1.2 points more often than their price said; elsewhere the differences are within what luck would give. The same holds at every level of play: the favourites' average closing price and their actual win rate are within 1.0 points of each other at each level.

Who beats it

Three kinds of account

Every account that bet before a match goes into one of three groups. Each account is sorted using only its bets in other matches, then scored on the match in question. A lucky match can't put an account in the winners' group for that same match, so the figures below are what each group really did.

D
Checks

How sure are these numbers?

  • Luck. 831 accounts cleared the bar over the year, but with 18,804 accounts tested, some cleared it by luck. That is why every edge on this page is measured on matches the account wasn't sorted on. It is what the group really earned, lucky members included.
  • Real money. At closing prices the clear winners were 1.3¢ ahead per $1. Their actual results after fees were +1.9¢ (likely range −1.0¢ to +4.3¢), and at Challenger and WTA 125 events +2.5¢ (+0.7¢ to +4.2¢). The closing price and the actual results point the same way.
  • Start times. Polymarket's scheduled start is not always the first serve. Taking the closing price 30 minutes earlier, and counting only bets placed before then, gives the same overall answer: clear winners +1.26¢, clear losers −1.39¢. By level the figures move within their ranges (table below).
  • Fees. Most fees had to be worked out from Polymarket's formula, because they are not in the trade records. The model was checked against the cash actually paid on 70 sampled bets, and it matched to within $0.0001 a share on 66.

E
Method and limits

How this was measured

  • Data. Every trade on Polymarket's tennis match-winner markets whose match was scheduled from 18 Sep 2025 to 21 Sep 2026: 36,037,873 records, both sides of each trade, each with its account. They come from Polymarket's public data (data-api.polymarket.com/trades), downloaded on 25 Sep 2026. 32,435 settled matches had a closing price, and 27,968 had bets before the start. Matches voided before play were left out.
  • Closing price. The midpoint between the best buy and sell prices in the last minute before the scheduled start, from Polymarket's price history.
  • A bet's edge. Shares × (closing price − price paid), for the player backed. Selling a player's shares counts as backing the opponent. Only bets at prices from 5¢ to 95¢ count. Both sides of every trade are included, so the edges add up to zero: what one account gains against the close, another gives up.
  • Sorting accounts. Matches were split into five groups. An account's group for a match comes from its bets in the other four. "Clearly" means its average edge over at least 10 other matches is more than twice the swing that luck alone would produce (two standard errors, with each match counted once).
  • Ranges. 95% ranges, from re-drawing whole matches 1,000 times.
  • Fees. From 28 Apr 2026, 5% (3% before July) × price × (1 − price) per share, paid when accepting a posted price. Earlier fees were taken in shares, which the trade records show. Before that, no fees were charged.
  • Levels of play. Taken from the tournament name. Men's and women's events are combined except in the men's table. ITF gender comes from the players' names (90% of ITF matches). Doubles, juniors and team events are in the full table but not the charts.

What this cannot say

  • An account is not a person. One bettor can use several accounts, and the groups describe accounts.
  • The closing price is right on average, not in every match. An account can beat the close in a match it then loses; the edge shows up over many bets.
  • "Value taken" is measured at closing prices, not in actual results, which are far noisier.
  • Scheduled start times. A match that started early would put a few bets placed during the match into the pre-match count; section D shows this does not change the answer.
  • Match-winner markets on international Polymarket only. Set, total and handicap markets, and Polymarket US, were not studied.
