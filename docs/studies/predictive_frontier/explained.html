Tennis research / explainedStudy completed 2 October 2026

A guide to what we learned

What improved in our tennis forecasts?

We found better ways to interpret a player’s recent performance, personal strengths and available history. That improved our own forecasts, especially for men.

The betting-market comparison is less encouraging: we have not established that these new ideas supply a reliable advantage over the odds.

01 / Start with the right comparison

We asked two different questions.

A forecast can be better than our previous model and still be worse than the betting market. So we tested both.

Test A · Our sporting model

Can we make our own forecast better?

Our starting model already combined player ratings with serve and return ability, recent form, workload, age and surface information.

We added the new signals and checked whether the resulting probabilities were better on the same matches.

Yes — a clear historical improvement for men.

Test B · The market-aware model

Can we add something the odds miss?

This starting model already knew the bookmaker’s odds, as well as our existing player-statistics profile.

We added the new signals again. This is a harder test: do they contribute anything beyond information already in that forecast?

No convincing new contribution established.

Elo is a strength rating that moves when a player wins or loses, taking the opponent’s strength into account. Our “Elo-based model” already contained several other useful inputs; this experiment improved on that stronger combination.

The closing line means the betting odds just before play begins. Odds can be converted into estimated winning chances. Here, historical prices from Pinnacle, a bookmaker, served as the main benchmark. Their exact quote times were not verified.

02 / The three most useful ideas

Better questions about the same players.

We researched 38 directions and tested five groups of new signals. These three gave the clearest individual improvements to the men’s sporting model.

The gain figures below are improvements in the probability score called “log loss.” The next section explains the units.

1

How much should we trust this player’s apparent ability?

+2.02men’s score gain

Two players can have similar recent numbers, but very different amounts of evidence behind them. A figure based on a handful of points deserves different treatment from one supported by a long, recent history.

An illustration, not a measured result

A small recent sampleA player looks strong, but we have seen little of their play.
A deep recent sampleAnother looks just as strong, backed by much more evidence.

The new signals track how much relevant point history we have and how variable the player’s performances have been. The model learns whether that should change its confidence in the forecast.

The old model already handled thin data to some extent. This adds another check when turning those estimates into a match prediction. It does not automatically push every uncertain forecast toward 50–50.

2

Is the player doing better than we expected from them?

+1.55men’s score gain

Winning lots of points is useful information. But we also need to ask who won them, and against whom. A great server doing something ordinary is a different signal from an average server suddenly exceeding expectations.

An invented example · points played while serving

Expected: 65 out of 100Based on the player’s own ability and this opponent’s returning ability.
Actual: 70 out of 100Five more points won than expected. A positive surprise.

We carry these surprises into future matches, giving recent performances more weight. We also compare short-term and longer-term patterns. A strong player winning 65 points could produce a negative surprise if we expected 70.

This improves on simply adjusting for the opponent: it asks whether the player exceeded their own expected performance against that opponent. Serving means starting the point; returning means receiving the opponent’s serve.

3

Is this surface a particular strength for this player?

+1.12men’s score gain

Tennis is played on hard courts, clay and grass. An overall rating cannot fully describe how each individual player performs in those different conditions.

An illustration

A player repeatedly serves and returns better on clay than the existing model expects. We give that player a modest clay-specific adjustment. With only a little clay history, the adjustment stays cautious.

The old model already knew about surfaces. The new feature asks a more personal question: does this player have a surface-specific pattern left over after those existing adjustments?

We tested a compact correction based on past performance surprises. We did not rebuild a full separate rating system for every player on every surface.

The other two ideas did not show a clear benefit on their own.

Recovery context · +0.24 for men

Gaps and interrupted matches

We used observed time since the last match and previous retirements — matches a player could not finish — alongside the player’s strength.

A gap in our records can mean missing data, rather than actual rest. This version’s evidence was inconclusive; it does not show that fatigue never matters.

Event conditions · +0.06 for men

Does this event favour a playing style?

We used serve results from earlier days at the same tournament to infer conditions, then related them to the players’ styles.

This was a rough conditions estimate, not a physical measurement of court speed. No clear individual benefit appeared in this test.

03 / Understanding the score

“Who wins?” is only part of a forecast.

“Alex has a 60% chance” and “Alex has a 90% chance” both pick Alex. But the second forecast makes a much stronger claim.

Our test rewards useful probabilities across many matches. The score, called log loss, gives a larger penalty when a model is very confident and wrong. Lower average loss is better. A single upset does not make a sensible forecast bad; the long-run pattern matters.

Why the small-looking numbers? We report improvements in milli-log-loss: 1 unit means a reduction of 0.001 in the average penalty. So the men’s combined gain of +3.68 corresponds to a reduction of about 0.003679.

That is a small improvement averaged over many matches. It does not convert directly into extra winners picked, a percentage increase in accuracy, or a betting profit.

04 / What actually happened

The men’s improvement is the stronger finding.

Men · 18,174 matches

All five groups of signals together

+3.68 milli-log-loss

Estimated uncertainty range: +2.41 to +4.94

A positive improvement over the strong existing model. It remained convincing after a stricter check for having tried many alternatives.

Women · 16,675 matches

A smaller, less secure gain

+1.18 milli-log-loss

Estimated uncertainty range: +0.30 to +2.06

The average result improved, but the evidence did not survive that same stricter check. Treat this as a lead to investigate.

For men, the combined model’s estimated gain was positive in all 8 evaluated years (2019–2026, with 2026 partial), although some individual years were too uncertain to stand on their own. From 2022 onward, its average gain was +4.09. The effect therefore was not limited to the oldest part of the test.

We also tried choosing each year’s model using only earlier seasons. That gained +3.43 for men. This is useful supporting evidence: the improvement did not depend solely on looking back and choosing the eventual winner.

The intervals shown are calculated for each comparison separately. The extra check across 28 comparisons is why some positive-looking intervals, especially for women, are still considered tentative.

How to read an uncertainty range

We only observed one set of historical matches. Another comparable set could give a different answer. The range expresses the uncertainty estimated from this sample.

If the range stretches from a negative number to a positive number, the data are compatible with a worse forecast, no change, or a better forecast. A range wholly above zero is encouraging, but is not a guarantee of future improvement.

Nearby matches can share conditions, so we grouped the uncertainty calculation into four-week calendar blocks. This still cannot account for every kind of dependence or for all the ideas investigated in earlier studies.

Why the gap calculation uses a different sample

The overall men’s improvement uses 18,174 matches. Only 16,362 also have the Pinnacle price needed for a fair head-to-head comparison.

On those shared matches, the new model improves by 3.437 milli-log-loss and the old gap was 24.170. Dividing those numbers gives 14.2%. This keeps the comparison on identical matches.

For reference, across the larger sporting-model sample the old average loss was 0.614337 and the new one was 0.610658.

05 / The harder test

Why this is not yet a betting edge.

Once the forecast already knew the odds and our existing player-statistics profile, none of the five new families showed a clear additional benefit.

One possible explanation is that the market already reflects much of the useful information. That is an interpretation of these results, rather than proof that bookmakers know every relevant fact.

There was one tempting hint.

For men, combining the odds, the old profile and the new reliability signals looked slightly better than the plain bookmaker probability. But we have to separate the old information from the new contribution.

The whole package vs plain odds

+0.86 milli-log-loss

Range: +0.05 to +1.68

Encouraging at first glance. However, this includes the existing statistics profile as well as the new signal, and it was one of several comparisons examined.

New reliability signal vs the old package

+0.23 milli-log-loss

Range: −0.04 to +0.50

This is the new contribution. Its uncertainty includes zero, so we cannot confidently say that the new signal improved the already market-aware forecast.

The conservative reading is that reliability deserves more study. We have not demonstrated a dependable new advantage over the closing line.

We evaluated prediction quality, not actual bets. Historical bookmaker records do not establish that those prices were available to trade at a known time. This study did not measure profit after spreads, fees or differences in retirement-settlement rules.

06 / How much confidence to place in it

A useful historical result, with real limits.

We tried to recreate what the model could know before a match, rather than letting later results sneak into earlier predictions.

  • We used corrected history. The earlier studies had data and timing problems; this experiment used their repaired inputs.
  • We checked more than the winning idea. All five families, their combination and a past-season selector were retained, including weak results. A stricter statistical check covered 28 main comparisons.
  • 39 software tests passed. These checked such things as whether future results can alter earlier predictions. They are implementation checks, not 39 separate demonstrations of a profitable model.

The main caution: this was not brand-new unseen history.

Earlier research had already examined these seasons. Although each prediction was fitted using earlier data, the overall research process had seen the historical results before. The next stronger test is genuinely new matches collected after the choices are fixed.

Other data limitations worth knowing

Incomplete match history. The accepted data mostly cover the main tours. Lower-level matches without trustworthy dates were excluded, and almost all of the men’s 2018 history is missing. Apparent inactivity may therefore be a recording gap.

A partial 2026 season. The sporting-model data reach August 2026, while the available Pinnacle prices end in mid-January 2026. These are properties of the stored dataset.

Dates, not exact publication times. New rolling signals use earlier days. The underlying repaired ratings can also use strictly earlier rounds of the same event. We do not have complete match-end, statistic-publication or quote timestamps.

Exploration creates false leads. Trying many ideas gives some a chance to look successful by luck. Our stricter check helps within this batch, but cannot undo every choice made during earlier research.

07 / What this suggests next

Keep improving what the numbers mean.

The most useful new signals did not rely on a flashy new data source. They asked whether a player was exceeding expectations, whether their surface history mattered personally, and how much evidence supported the forecast.

Reasonable next experiments are a fuller player-by-surface model and ways to carry uncertainty all the way into the final winning chance. More detailed data we already hold could also support studies of serve placement, return weaknesses and different rally lengths — the number of shots in a point.

Those are suggestions, not additional successful findings from this experiment. Detailed shot-data features were researched but not tested in this batch.

The practical finding: our men’s sporting model made better probability estimates using these signals in this historical test. Finding a dependable advantage over the betting market remains an open research question.
