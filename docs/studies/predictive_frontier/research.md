# Predictive frontier: research and candidate register

2 October 2026. Research evidence and proposed experiments, not a model selection or betting decision. External sources were accessed on **2026-10-02**. This note records the brainstorm independently of which candidates ultimately score well; measured results belong in the accompanying experiment report.

The most promising immediate work is to improve what the existing statistics mean: **performance relative to the player's own expected level, player-specific surface adaptation, and the reliability of the available history**. Those can be tested on the corrected histories now. Richer rally and serve-placement information is already present elsewhere in this repository, but joining it safely is a separate task. Nothing in the literature reviewed establishes that these features beat this repository's closing-price benchmark.

## Starting point and what is genuinely new

The [repaired study](../feature_alpha_audit/repairs.md) is the numerical starting point. It finds a large gain from serve/return information over Elo, while the combined non-market model still trails Pinnacle. Its men's recent-statistics profile has a positive but uncertain increment beyond the calibrated line. Those findings motivate better representations; they do not establish that pre-match information has reached a forecasting ceiling.

The earlier [candidate register](../predictability_diagnostic/candidates.md) already covered ordinary serve/return percentages, aces, double faults, first-serve percentage, first/second-serve success, hold/break rates, sets/games/points won, tiebreaks, deciding sets, head-to-head, handedness, rank, age, height, entry status, home country, broad recent workload, surface familiarity, and margin-weighted Elo. The [UFC study](../ufc_ideas/README.md) added Bradley–Terry, market-derived skill, adjusted performance, IOS, schedule strength and coarse fatigue. Recommending these raw columns again would mostly repeat previous work. Weak families were not all rebuilt after the chronology repair, so their old scores are not corrected negative evidence.

Two previously suggested but untested directions must also be described honestly: the [audit's Elo notes](../feature_alpha_audit/elo_notes.md) already proposed player-by-surface serve/return ratings, and the repair report already proposed own-expected performance. They are **new experiments here**, not ideas first invented in this brainstorm.

Source inspection explains the distinction. The repaired `adjperf` profile reuses the opponent-relative construction documented at the top of [`stageB_adjperf.py`](../../../src/eda/ufc_ideas/stageB_adjperf.py): a statistic is compared with the opponent's allowed norm. It does not directly subtract the joint prediction from both players' latent strengths. The serve/return model has global player effects and a tour/surface intercept; that differs from a separate, partially pooled surface deviation for each player. A dependable server can produce an ordinary positive opponent-adjusted score while simultaneously performing below their own level.

**Availability classes below:** **A** uses the repaired history or modest transformations of its existing inputs; **B** needs a new join to data catalogued and present on disk; **C** needs additional information or an availability audit before a historical experiment. Availability means the inputs may be buildable, not that their incremental value is established. Every candidate is a suggestion unless explicitly labelled as a local observation or a published result.

## Primary-source evidence that changes priorities

The sources support different claims. Forecasting studies support predictive designs; physiology studies support possible mechanisms; data documentation supports field availability. Those are not interchangeable forms of evidence.

The highest-priority evidence is S1–S4: improve estimation of player ability before adding a long list of weak narratives. S5–S9 motivate conditional styles and point structure. S10–S12 warrant measured context tests, with no assumed sign or automatic betting interpretation.

## Candidate register: repaired histories first

The sixteen A candidates are transformations or extensions, not sixteen independent discoveries. Related rows should be grouped into a few experiments; searching every window and interaction separately would inflate the apparent evidence.

| ID | Candidate and concrete construction | Difference from previous work; reason to test | Priority / main risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A01 | **Own-expected serve/return innovations.** For historical match h, residual = observed serve wins minus expected wins from the pre-h serve, opposing return and surface model; aggregate residual sums divided by shrunk exposure. Repeat for return. | Existing opponent-relative form mixes stable player ability with changes in that ability. The proposed residual isolates unexpected performance. S1/S2 motivate joint expectations; this exact feature is a proposal already named in the audit. | First; expectation must be genuinely pre-match and frozen before observing h. |
| A02 | **Fast versus slow form.** Short-memory innovation minus a longer-memory innovation, with a slow strength input retained. | Two players with equal recent adjusted averages may have different trajectories. Previous studies chose a short half-life, but did not isolate persistent level from a temporary departure. | First, grouped with A01; few fixed windows, selected only before evaluation. |
| A03 | **Partially pooled player-by-surface innovations.** A player's global serve/return level plus a shrunk deviation for clay, hard or grass, decaying with age of observations. | Current global strength plus surface intercept cannot express different player adaptations. Earlier surface percentages and surface Elo cover related information but not this point-model representation. S2/S3 directly motivate it. | First; grass sparsity and overfitting. An incremental residual implementation is a screening approximation to a full joint model. |
| A04 | **Evidence reliability.** Point exposure, effective sample size and age of the most recent statistic, interacting with signed model strength and disagreement. | Equal feature values from one match and twenty matches should not necessarily imply equal confidence. Existing shrinkage addresses estimation; downstream reliability interactions test whether the remaining certainty is appropriate. | First; missing history is source coverage, not automatically inactivity. |
| A05 | **Scoring-conversion residual.** Previous match/game result minus the iid expectation implied by that historical match's point rates; shrink and decay. | Separates strong underlying play from unusually favourable conversion of points into games/sets/wins. Existing games-weighted Elo and break-point-over-expectation cover parts, but not this explicit result-versus-point decomposition. | Next batch; historical realized rates may be used only after that match, and need sampling-error correction. Not tested in the first batch. |
| A06 | **As-of event playing speed × style.** Estimate event ace residuals from prior editions and earlier eligible days of the current edition, adjusted for both players; interact with serve-versus-return strength. | Goes beyond hard/clay/grass and an unconditional ace-rate difference. S2/S5 motivate event heterogeneity. A shared condition alone does not tell us which player benefits. | First/second; same-day ordering, changing venues/balls, and thin early-round estimates. |
| A07 | **Separate current-event and pre-event activity.** Split load into matches/points accumulated in this draw and activity before arriving. | Fourteen-day sets conflate recent fitness, prior workload and surviving the current tournament. New decomposition of an existing useful feature, not a new fatigue claim. | First/second; confounding with round and strength remains. |
| A08 | **Actual surface-transition recency.** Days since last accepted match on today's surface; whether the immediately previous match used another surface; number of recent same-surface matches. | The old flag is fewer than three surface matches in the calendar year. A December hard-court match and a clay-to-grass switch need different interpretations. | First/second; dates are days, not exact recovery times; missing lower-tier history. |
| A09 | **Load × recovery × age.** Allow acute points/minutes load to have a different effect after one day and after several days, with modest age interaction. | Previous workload and age were mainly additive; a heavily loaded player with time off is different from one playing the next day. S10 motivates the interaction, not its sign. | Second; rest-hour precision unavailable; avoid inventing recovery timestamps. |
| A10 | **Performance volatility.** Shrunk variance of own-expected residuals after accounting for binomial point noise; interact symmetric volatility with the baseline logit. | Most existing features estimate means. Extra performance uncertainty may change favourite confidence even if the mean skill is unchanged. This is an original modelling hypothesis. | Second; uncertainty about the estimate and genuine match-to-match variability must stay distinct. |
| A11 | **Cold-start prior from rank and career stage.** Use prior rank/points and age to initialize or condition weak-history ratings. | Rank was tested as a generic input; its usefulness may concentrate when repaired histories are sparse or players return from long gaps. | Second; restored reliable lower-level dates would be preferable to pretending missing history is novice status. |
| A12 | **Nonlinear strength and style interactions.** A small symmetric feature set or a carefully constrained nonlinear residual model. | Linear A−B inputs discard some information in the two absolute profiles. Example: S_A R_B − S_B R_A captures an antisymmetric cross-interaction, unlike merely re-adding S_A−S_B and R_A−R_B. | Second; iid matchup already captures some nonlinearity, so measure incremental value over the full baseline. |
| A13 | **Integrate uncertainty through the point-to-match model.** Average match probability over distributions of serve/return abilities, rather than insert only their means. | The iid conversion is nonlinear. Plug-in and averaged probabilities can differ even with the same central skill estimate. S2/S4 motivate uncertainty modelling; the exact gain is unknown. | Second; computationally heavier and vulnerable to a misspecified variance model. |
| A14 | **Completion risk as a separate target component.** Model prior own retirements, gaps and completed-match performance jointly with win-if-completed. | A past retirement flag was tested; a two-component advancing probability is different. It could explain errors that a completed-match skill model cannot. | Second; match advancement and a venue's settled contract can differ. No inference that an individual is injured without evidence. |
| A15 | **Component change before results change.** Track unusual changes in first-serve-in rate, double faults, first-serve success and return success relative to each player's slow mean. | A recent multidimensional profile exists, but its innovations relative to the player's own baseline may detect a change earlier and with less stable-style contamination. | Second, an extension of A01; correlated inputs and many implicit change-point tests. |
| A16 | **Previous market surprise versus previous point surprise.** Contrast historical result minus historical market probability with historical point-model residuals. | Existing MESR learns a level from prices; this asks whether recent price errors were supported by underlying play. Today's line is never replaced by a future price. | Second/low; narrow paired price coverage, duplicated information, and historical quotes lack precise availability times. |

Even a match-by-match rebuild can still use a chart that was created years later; use first-publication/commit evidence where available or explicitly label the result a retrospective information study.

## A bounded first experiment batch

The finalized first experiment protocol groups the shortlist into five families: **own-expected innovations**, **surface specialization**, **reliability**, **recovery context**, and **event playing speed**. The first two directly address known restrictions in a useful existing signal. Reliability tests evidence depth and staleness; recovery treats rest, prior activity and retirement information jointly, rather than relabelling the already tested raw rest feature as new. Event speed tests the conditions/style hypothesis. The groups use market-free sporting inputs. Their combined model and a selector using prior-season performance are also evaluated. Scoring-conversion residuals and the granular shot-data candidates remain follow-up suggestions and must not be described as tested here.

A full Bayesian state-space rebuild, a large neural network, and dozens of hand-written motivational narratives are less useful first experiments because their complexity would obscure what supplied any gain. The shortlist uses compact approximations that can fail even if a more complete model would work; a negative result only applies to the implementation actually measured.

The main comparisons use (a) the strong nine-input repaired Elo-based combination, and (b) the Pinnacle model with the existing recent profile and context. The men's market model replicates the repaired comparator; its women's counterpart is an analogous specification, not a previously established WTA winner. Average-price comparisons provide sensitivity. A plain calibrated market remains a useful reference, and the complete non-market forecast should also be compared with **raw** Pinnacle on identical matches. Beating a weaker Elo baseline, calibrating odds, and finding information conditional on the odds are different claims. The companion experiment report is the authority for the exact scored cohorts and comparisons.

Features should be fixed before examining the new evaluation results. Fit scaling, regression weights and any penalty choices only on earlier seasons. If a development interval selects families, reserve a later interval for the selected joint model and show the entire candidate ledger. These calendar holdouts are holdouts for the current fitting procedure; prior studies already examined later outcomes, so they are not an untouched discovery holdout. New future data are needed for that claim.

Use paired per-match log-loss differences in milli-log-loss, calendar-block uncertainty and the same eligible rows for each baseline/candidate comparison. Report ATP and WTA separately. The relevant checks are chronology under future-data mutation, side-swap complementarity, train-only scaling and tuning, row coverage, and the response of later forecasts to the same mutation. Conditional subgroup scores are diagnostics; choosing a winning event, surface or player group after inspecting them creates another selection step.

Keep retirement handling and match format aligned with the repaired study, and preserve its exclusions. The repaired histories establish day-level ordering, with earlier rounds of the same event handled under that study's rule; they do not establish exact publication or executable quote times. A small log-loss gain is a probabilistic forecasting result, not an after-cost return or a reason to place a bet.

## Data rights and reproducibility boundaries

No new sporting, odds, weather, wearable or news dataset was downloaded in this research. No private biometric records were requested. Source access was public literature and documentation, including indexed text when a direct publisher fetch failed. The literature's data and code rights are separate from the right to read the paper.

The catalog pins MCP to commit 1813a13 and the Sackmann archival results mirror to commit 8373358. Actual experiment manifests should carry the complete file hashes of the repaired inputs and any newly joined files. The repair report is authoritative for its rebuilt sample; catalog descriptions alone must not become sample-count claims for a new experiment.

MCP and relevant Sackmann archives are documented as CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. Other held feeds have missing or conflicting data licences; code licences do not automatically cover provider data. Tennis-Data's current private-use wording is restrictive. These observations preserve provenance and identify limits on reuse; no commercial/product permission is inferred. Historical chart publication dates, historical weather forecast availability, and synchronized executable closing prices remain unverified inputs for the richer branches above.
