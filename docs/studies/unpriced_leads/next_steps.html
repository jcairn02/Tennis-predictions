What this is about

The study found five patterns in 2013–2024 matches that the closing odds seemed to miss:

Luck
Players whose recent results were luckier than their play (for example, on break points or in close sets) are overrated.
Post-Slam slump
Players who reached a Grand Slam quarter-final or better do worse than expected for about three weeks afterwards.
Big servers on fast courts
They are underrated.
Reputation
Seeded and famous players are overrated. Players coming up with good results from lower-level events are underrated.
Rhythm
The odds don't fully count doubles played the week before, very short rest, or a heavy recent workload.

The idea is to add these as one small adjustment on top of the model's prediction, the same way the serve-speed adjustment works now. The main model stays as it is. Below are the steps needed before that adjustment can be trusted with real money.

Stage 1

Before we look at any 2025–26 results

Can start now
  1. 1

    Write the rules down before we look.

    Once we've seen the results, we'd be tempted to bend the rules to fit them. So we decide now:

    • One adjustment, not five. Each pattern alone is too weak to judge. Combined, they have a fair chance of giving a clear answer.
    • Size it from the 2025–26 results, not the old ones. The old numbers overstate the patterns. We picked the best five out of about 500, so part of their strength was luck. Sized from the old numbers, the adjustment would do more harm than good, even if the patterns are as real as we expect.
    • Put a limit on it. The post-Slam slump affects only about 2 matches in 100, but strongly. A limit stops one pattern from swinging a single prediction too far.
    • The pass mark. Use the adjustment only if the 2025–26 results still lean clearly the right way. A real pattern passes about 7 times in 10. A dead one passes by luck about 1 time in 6.
  2. 2

    Build it into the prediction code.

    • It goes next to the serve-speed adjustment, and both are worked out together.
    • If a pattern can't be calculated for a match because data is missing, it adds nothing.
    • We check that it doesn't double-count serve speed. The fast-court pattern is about serving, so the two may overlap.
    • We do a test run on 2020–24 only to make sure the code works. Those seasons were used to find the patterns, so any gain there proves nothing.
  3. 3

    Make sure the live numbers match the historical ones.

    • Right now, study code calculates the patterns on past matches. The daily prediction run has to calculate them for upcoming matches.
    • To prove the daily version is right, we replay some past days as if they were live and check the numbers come out exactly the same. The project already does this check for the model's other inputs.
    • The fast-court pattern estimates court speed from other players' aces at the same event. It must use only matches that had definitely finished before the match being predicted. This is the project's existing rule against peeking at later results.
  4. 4

    Fix the data gaps.

    Without these, only part of the adjustment can run:

    • Match statistics at every level dropped sharply from mid-2026. In July they were at 40% of the year before for men and 19% for women. The luck pattern needs them, so it stays off until they're back.
    • Lower-level results are missing: women's ITF qualifying since 2025, and most lower-level events since June 2026. The "coming up from lower levels" half of the reputation pattern needs them. Without them, that half is off for about 70% of women's matches. Adding this data needs your OK, because the project's rule is that new lower-level data is tested before it's accepted.
    • Live doubles results and match start times. The rhythm pattern needs them at the time we bet. We already have a collector that can be extended.

    Today, only the post-Slam slump, big servers on fast courts, and the "seeded and famous" half of the reputation pattern could run.

  5. 5

    Optional, costs money (your call): Pinnacle's closing odds from August 2025.

    • From September 2025, the only closing odds we have are BetOnline's. Those are softer than Pinnacle's, especially for women's matches.
    • So a pattern can look like it beats the odds just because BetOnline's odds are soft.
    • The free alternative: the check reports the months with Pinnacle odds separately. That shows whether the patterns also beat the sharper odds.
Stage 2

The check

Needs your go
  1. 6

    Run the check on every match since January 2025.

    That's about 9,500 matches, and it takes minutes. We apply the rules from step 1 and look at three things:

    • How much of the patterns' old strength is still there.
    • How much they add on top of our model's own predictions, not just on top of the odds.
    • Whether they hold in the months with Pinnacle odds, not just the BetOnline months.

    The old list ran this only on matches up to 28 July 2026 and saved the rest for 2027.

Stage 3

After the check, before real bets

Before real money
  1. 7

    Measure it against the odds you actually bet at.

    • The check compares against the closing odds, but you bet earlier, at a specific venue.
    • The odds move during the day: toward some of these patterns and away from others. So the edge when you bet isn't the same as the edge at the close.
    • Part of the luck pattern's effect comes from matches where a player retired. If your venue cancels bets when a player retires, that part doesn't pay. In that case, size the luck pattern on finished matches only.
  2. 8

    Run the project's standard before-and-after comparison, as a safety check only.

    The expected gain is too small for this comparison to detect, but it can catch real damage.

  3. 9

    Keep stakes small at first.

    Until the adjustment has proven itself on new matches, it shouldn't raise bet sizes much. For example, bets that exist only because of the adjustment get smaller stakes. This is a standing project rule.

Stage 4

Once it's live

Every month
  1. 10

    Re-check every month.

    As new results come in, we update the size. It grows if the patterns keep working and shrinks toward zero if they don't. Markets learn, and two patterns (luck and the post-Slam slump) were already fading in 2023–24, so watch those two closely.

    The old list had two steps about waiting until August 2027. This monthly re-check replaces them.
