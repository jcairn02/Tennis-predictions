## Your role: B1, the rhythm-and-body scout

Role name for your files: `rhythm_body` (memo `docs/studies/unpriced_leads/team/rhythm_body.md`, CSV `docs/studies/unpriced_leads/team/rhythm_body_ideas.csv`, id prefix `RB`, scratch `data/studies/unpriced_leads/team/rhythm_body/`).

**Your lens: how ready a player is right now, physically and rhythmically, beyond what the model's coarse schedule inputs already say.** edge_anatomy found the schedule-and-body family (`sets_14d`, `surf_season`, `surf_layoff`, `age`) the backbone of the unpriced edge: small effects with the same sign in 9–10 of 10 tour-eras since 2013, and the one family the model cannot do without. Those inputs are crude counts. The other LLM's advice is "richer rhythm and rust statistics". Find the richer information: what a coach, a physio or a sharp tennis trader knows about readiness that a set count, a day count and a birth date do not.

Angles to consider, then go beyond them (each must become a computable statistic):
* **Rest in hours, not days:** start times and durations (the clock's `start`, `minutes`, the Slam and WTA point clocks), late finishes followed by early starts, the turnaround after a night match, the opponent's rest in the same round.
* **The physical cost of recent matches, not their count:** points or minutes played this event and in the last 7/14 days, three-setters, tiebreaks, deciding sets, the previous match's length against the opponent's; versus `sets_14d`, which counts sets and includes this event (so it partly measures progress through the draw). Separating *rhythm* (matches won here, on-site adaptation, U-138) from *fatigue* (minutes, points) may itself be new.
* **Week to week:** consecutive weeks played, travel distance and time zones crossed (a venue gazetteer), continent and surface switches, indoor/outdoor switches, altitude changes (Bogotá, Quito, Gstaad, Kitzbühel, Madrid), the expanded 12-day events (a seed's bye against an opponent who has already played there).
* **Load the singles history does not see:** qualifying rounds (qualifiers and lucky losers arrive with 2–3 matches), doubles and mixed doubles at the same event, team events (Davis Cup, BJK Cup, United Cup, Laver Cup), exhibitions.
* **Health signals:** retirements and walkovers given recently at any level, medical time-outs (the WTA umpire log), withdrawals from events entered (entry lists against appearances), protected rankings, time away from any competition (not only from the surface: U-132 days since the last match, any level, is built), the first matches back.
* **The body over a career:** age against the player's own career curve (rising, peak, declining), career match odometer, years on tour, the season's odometer late in the year (U-144), height × load.
* **Season phase and conditions:** the first event of the year, the autumn, heat (wet-bulb globe temperature for outdoor day matches, needs weather data) and a player's own heat tolerance from past hot matches, humidity, wind.
* **Stamina as a trait or a state:** how a player's serve and return numbers hold up in later sets against his own early sets, in recent matches against his norm (point data where available).

**Check the pool before proposing.** Many load and schedule ideas are built and screened: `universe.csv` rows U-128–U-162, the feature-interactions blocks B-LOAD, B-PREV, B10 (arrival: travel, deep run, consecutive weeks, jet lag), B11 (on-site rhythm), B-INJ (rust after a break), B9a (constitution × load), B-MOTIV, B6, I-053, and their results in `docs/studies/feature_interactions/` and `next_candidates.md`. Days-based fatigue and travel were unresolved beyond the close. If you re-propose one, say what is new: better data (hours instead of days, real distances, doubles), a better construction, or a different population (qualifiers, the 12-day events).

Rank your ideas by your judgment of (a) a plausible effect beyond the 2025–26 close, (b) a mechanism the market would miss, (c) data in hand or cheaply obtainable, (d) availability at bet time.
