## Your role: R1, the researcher (literature and data sources)

Role name for your files: `researcher` (memo `docs/studies/unpriced_leads/team/researcher.md`, CSV `docs/studies/unpriced_leads/team/researcher_ideas.csv`, id prefix `RS`, scratch `data/studies/unpriced_leads/team/researcher/`). Use the web (WebSearch, WebFetch) and the repository.

**Two jobs.**

**1. The evidence.** What does published work (peer-reviewed papers, working papers, serious practitioner analysis such as Tennis Abstract's blog, Kovalchik's work, betting-market studies) say about predictors of professional tennis results **beyond the betting market** (best) or beyond rankings and ratings (weaker), and about which of them the market prices? Cover at least: fatigue, rest and scheduling (incl. night sessions, time of day); travel and jet lag; home advantage and crowds; heat, humidity, wind, altitude; balls and court speed; break-point, tiebreak and "clutch" performance as skill or luck; deviations from the iid point model; hot hand and momentum; favourite–longshot and other betting-market biases in tennis; market efficiency of tennis closing lines; retirements, injuries and returns; age and career curves; gender differences under pressure; post-Slam letdown and pre-Slam effort; qualifiers; lower-level results. For each source: the claim, the effect size and its unit, the data and seasons, whether it was measured against market prices, its type (peer-reviewed / working paper / practitioner / provider claim), whether you read the full text or the abstract only, URL and access date. Be candid about what is weak or old (markets learn: an effect found against 2005 prices may be priced by 2025).

**2. The partial-information sources.** For candidate statistics the project holds only partly or not at all, find where the missing data can be had: hourly weather and wet-bulb globe temperature (Open-Meteo's archive and others), a venue gazetteer with coordinates and altitude, court pace indices by edition, ball brand by event, order of play and real start times, doubles and mixed results, medical time-outs and retirements, withdrawals and entry lists, coaching changes, Hawk-Eye tracking, women's serve speeds, Match Charting Project updates. For each: the field, its coverage (tours, levels, seasons), the access route, its terms (public / login / paywall / CAPTCHA), the cost, and whether the scraping approval of 8 Oct 2026 covers it (`docs/decisions/2026-10-08-scraping-approved.md`: public data and the sites' own decryption yes; others' logins, paywalls, CAPTCHAs no; paid data is the owner's call). A few probe requests to confirm a field exists are fine (receipts in your scratch folder); no bulk download.

**Read first, and do not duplicate:** `docs/research/README.md` and the notes it lists (granular-data-leads, gap-sources, serve-speed-sources, early-lines, match-timing, market-anchoring, rating-systems, point-shot-ideas), `docs/studies/edge_anatomy/team/practitioner.md` § 3 (its literature list) and `team/tennis_scout.md` "Sources". Build on them; cite them where they already settle a point.

In your memo, end with the five research-backed candidate statistics you judge most likely to be unpriced by the 2025–26 close, and put every research-backed candidate in your `ideas.csv` (same columns as the others; `prior_evidence` carries the citation).
