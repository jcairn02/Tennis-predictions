One model beat the 2026 closing line, narrowly, on inputs with a known timing leak

A lead, not yet an edge

From the tabpfn_thinking study (2 Oct 2026). Over the whole 2022–2026 test it gained +1.57 (95% interval +0.82 to +2.32) for men, positive in every season: 2022 +1.24, 2023 +0.72, 2024 +1.30, 2025 +2.28, 2026 +2.70.

Why it is not settled
  1. Built on the original features. The feature audit later found those leak some results across overlapping tournaments. On the repaired features the simple linear version of this model fell from +0.83 to +0.25. The paid TabPFN fit has not been rerun on repaired features.
  2. The closing odds are an input. The model learns how far to move off the market price. It is a "market plus adjustment" model, not a standalone handicapper.
  3. One fit, one seed, a looked-at test. Its inputs were chosen after earlier studies had seen 2022–2026, so 2026 is not an untouched holdout. The UFC study saw about 1 milli-log-loss between TabPFN versions on the same inputs.

In UFC terms: your 2025 v2 kept the odds out and let MESR carry the market. This model instead takes the closing line itself as a column and learns where its other features say the line is wrong. Its whole edge is the size of that correction.

What the number measures

Log loss averages −ln(probability you gave the eventual winner). A coin scores 0.693; lower is better. One milli-log-loss is 0.001. It is Brier's stricter cousin: confident misses cost much more.

The 2026 numbers behind it

Men's average closing line: 0.5929. Recalibrated close: 0.5925. TabPFN with odds: 0.5898. Surface Elo alone trails the close by 19.5 milli-log-loss on the same matches.

What it is not

A log-loss gain is not a betting return. No study has yet turned any of these forecasts into stakes, prices taken, fees or closing-line value. The close is used here as a forecaster to beat.

3 · The model at a glance

What it is, what it learned from, what it was tested on

The model

TabPFN-3.5-Thinking, Prior Labs' hosted tabular foundation model. It trains no weights: it reads the training rows as context and predicts the test rows in one pass. "Thinking" mode spends extra compute searching preprocessing and ensembles.

Settings copied from the UFC production fit: 8 estimators, softmax temperature 0.9, seed 0, log-loss objective. One paid fit: 162,211 tokens, 275 seconds.

Trained on

39,918 main-tour singles matches from 2013–2021 that have closing odds: 19,331 men's and 20,587 women's, pooled with a tour column.

  • Main tour = Grand Slams, Tour Finals, 1000s, 500s, 250s (the only matches with public closing odds).
  • No men's 2018: the odds file for that season is missing.
  • 2010–2012 left out while every rating warms up.
Tested on

2022–2026: 12,352 men's and 11,467 women's matches with odds. The 2026 slice is 1,774 men's and 1,714 women's matches, events starting 4 January to 28 July.

  • Test outcomes were never uploaded to the API.
  • Inputs keep updating from earlier matches during the test; the fitted model does not.
One row = one match

Player A is chosen at random (a hash of the match id). The target is "A won"; after a retirement, the player who advanced won. Every player input is A minus B.

Each test match is predicted twice, as stored and with A and B swapped, and the two forecasts averaged. Same idea as your corner-agnostic augmentation.

Where the data comes from

  • Jeff Sackmann's tennis_atp / tennis_wta files: the base. Results, scores, box scores (aces, double faults, serve points won and so on), rankings, age, height, hand, country. 461,670 men's and 405,621 women's matches from 2010 at every level: tour, Challenger and WTA 125, ITF, qualifying.
  • Tennis My Life: 5,413 matches Sackmann lacks, mostly after 25 May 2026, where his tour files stop.
  • tennis-data.co.uk: closing odds for tour-level main draws. The average of several bookmakers (from Oddsportal) and Pinnacle's, captured 3 Aug 2026. 99.93% of rows linked to a match.
  • Hand-made tables: level mapping, each Grand Slam's deciding-set rule, each event's country.

History for the features uses every level. The model itself trains and tests only on main-tour matches, because only those have public odds.

The time rule, and why tennis makes it hard

A feature for a match may use only earlier events and earlier rounds of the same event. In UFC a card is one night. A tennis event runs one or two weeks, rounds fall on different days, several events overlap, and the public files give only the event's start date and the round, not the day a match was played.

The original features ordered history by event start date. Late rounds of a two-week event could then reach another event that started during it, and some helpers read the match format from the final score. The repair kept only matches with a provider-dated match day. Those exist only for the main tour, so history fell from 872,704 matches to 78,714.

Your v2 then: AutoGluon 1.4 on a blue-minus-red frame, Brier-tuned Bayesian decayed averages, online IOS, MESR, time-aware TrueSkill, Platt calibration, a single time-ordered holdout.

1input The closing oddsThe betting market's own probability. Everything else in the model is a correction to it. Leaned on0.31
odds_logit

Closing odds, as log-odds that A wins

How it's built
  1. Take the average closing decimal odds across bookmakers for each player (tennis-data.co.uk's AvgW/AvgL).
  2. Invert to implied probabilities, then divide by their sum. That strips the bookmaker margin: the median pair sums to 1.058, a 5.8% overround.
  3. Drop incoherent pairs (sum outside 1.00–1.20, e.g. swapped values) and odds of 0 or 1.00.
  4. Convert A's probability to log-odds.
Data
tennis-data.co.uk, one workbook per tour and season. The provider says the odds are the most recent before play; that they are true closing odds is not verified.
UFC analogue
Your final_odds, de-vigged the same way, but used as a model input. In 2025 your v2 used it only for EV and bet rules, with MESR as the market's input.
Evidence
Ladder: the 100% markRecalibration alone: +0.51 men / +0.49 women (2022–26)Fitted slope 1.09–1.10

A slope above 1 means the average line is slightly under-confident: favourites win a little more often than priced.

1input Surface EloThe classic win/loss rating, blended with a rating earned on the match's own surface. Leaned on0.12
elo_logit

Surface Elo's log-odds that A wins

Tennis is played on hard courts, clay (slow, high bounce) and grass (fast, low bounce). Some players are much better on one. So each player keeps an overall rating and one per surface, and the forecast blends them.

How it's built
  1. Every player starts at 1,500. History: every singles match since 2010 at every level, 872,704 matches. Retirements count as results; walkovers never move a rating.
  2. After each match, move both ratings by K × (result − expected). K = k0 / (matches played + offset)shape, so newcomers move fast. Tuned on 2013–2018: k0 600, offset 2, shape 0.6 for men (offset 1 for women).
  3. Keep a separate rating per surface, moved only by matches on it.
  4. Forecast with (1 − w) × overall + w × surface, w = 0.30 (men), 0.25 (women).
  5. Time rule: rating at the start of the week, plus what this event's earlier rounds added.
Data
Results from Sackmann plus Tennis My Life.
UFC analogue
Your v1 Elo (fixed K 96) with two upgrades: K shrinks as a player gains experience, and the surface split is like rating a fighter separately at another weight, partly pooled with the main rating.
Evidence
Alone: 81.7 / 83.8 better than a coin (2022–26, matches with odds)78% / 82% of the way to the closeSurface blend vs plain Elo: +3.8 / +3.0

The rating-systems study (3 Oct) found TrueSkill Through Time beats this Elo by 2.65 / 3.00 and named it the leading base rating, but no rating adds anything once the odds are known.

2inputs Serve/return ratings and games-weighted EloStrength measured in points won on serve and on return, and an Elo that credits the margin of victory. Leaned on0.09
ratings_iid

Serve and return ratings, turned into a match probability

The single strongest addition to Elo in any study. Each player has a serve rating S and a return rating R. The chance the server wins a point is modelled as expit(μ + Sserver − Rreturner), where μ is the tour's serve-point average on that surface (men: 62.8% hard, 60.4% clay, 65.5% grass).

How it's built
  1. After each completed match with a box score, compare points won on serve with the expected share. The surplus moves the server's S up and the returner's R down, scaled by each rating's uncertainty (a Glicko-style Gaussian update on point counts).
  2. Uncertainty shrinks with every point and grows with weeks away. Constants tuned on 2013–2018 per tour (e.g. a newcomer's serve starts 0.20 below their return for men).
  3. Feed both players' expected serve-point shares into the iid point model: treat every point as an independent draw and compute exactly the chance of winning games, sets and the match. It knows best-of-3 versus best-of-5 and each Slam's deciding-set rule.
  4. The input is that match probability as log-odds.
Data
Box scores (Sackmann, TML), every level from 2010: 197,673 men's and 89,115 women's matches move the ratings.
UFC analogue
Like rating striking offence and striking defence from strike counts, then simulating the fight from them rather than from the W/L.

Games-weighted Elo

The same Elo, except each update is multiplied by the winner's share of the games played (rescaled to average 1). A 6-0 6-1 win moves ratings more than 7-6 7-6. This is the "WElo" of Angelini, Candila and De Angelis (2022).

adjperf_tpw_2w

Opponent-adjusted total points won, two-week memory

How it's built
  1. Opponent's norm. For each opponent, what their own earlier opponents managed against them on total points won (log-odds scale), as a weighted mean with its spread (mean absolute deviation). Memory 104 weeks, shrunk toward the tour average with a prior of 2 matches (men).
  2. Per-match z. (Player's points-won log-odds − opponent's norm) / opponent's spread, clipped to ±4.
  3. Player's form. Recency-weighted average of their z over earlier matches, half-life 2 weeks, pulled toward 0 with a prior of 10 matches.
Data
Box scores, every level from 2010 (573,142 player-matches with a box score).
UFC analogue
The same per-fight formula as your v1 adjperf on SS.HEAD_% and SS.GROUND_%, rebuilt from the UFC code. The tuned memory is far shorter: tennis players play most weeks, and 2 weeks beat every other half-life tried, from 1 week to no decay.
Evidence
Given Elo: +3.19 / +3.32Given odds: +0.14 / −0.07Career-long version: worse than Elo alone
ios_points_short

IOS points matchup, short memory

How it's built
  1. Every earlier match says: server's serve-points-won log-odds ≈ μ + Oserver − Dreturner.
  2. Solve all players' offence O and defence D jointly by ridge regression over every earlier week, older matches decayed with a half-life of 4 weeks (men) or 13 weeks (women). Re-solved each week, plus this event's earlier rounds.
  3. A's expected serve share against B and B's against A go through the iid point model; the input is the match probability as log-odds.
UFC analogue
Your ios_ idea (μ + offence − opponent's defence) on serve points. Your 2025 version updated online after each fight with a ~557-day half-life; this one is the joint ridge solve your UFC IOS became in 2026, with a very short memory.

16inputs Two-week statistics profileHow each player has served and returned over the last fortnight, statistic by statistic, scored against what those opponents usually allow. Leaned on0.44

This family is the one closing-line lead the studies agree on: per-statistic form for men, independent of every rating. It is the thing your edge thesis asked for: offensive and defensive ratings of individual statistics, not a new win rating.

The shared recipe (adjperf and adjdef, steps 1–3)

adjperf
The player's statistic in each earlier match, as a z against what that opponent usually allows: (value − opponent's norm) / opponent's spread, clipped ±4. Averaged with a 2-week half-life and pulled toward 0.
adjdef
How far the player held each opponent below the opponent's own usual value. In tennis, adjdef on a serve statistic equals adjperf on the matching return statistic, so it is kept only for the four statistics with no return mirror.
Constants
Serve-side skills use serve-points constants (men: opponent memory 104 weeks, prior 2; form prior 10 matches). Return-side skills use return-points constants (men: memory 52 weeks; form prior 160 matches, close to a plain decayed sum). Rates on the log-odds scale; break points faced per game on the log scale. Every match counts equally: weighting by points played (your τ exponent) lost on every grid.
UFC analogue
Your v1 adjperf/adjdef pair, applied to every serve and return statistic instead of two striking percentages.
Data
Box scores, every level from 2010. Retirements and non-standard formats are left out of the history.

21inputs Long-memory IOS profile and matchup productsStable serve-side traits: an offence and a defence rating per statistic, solved jointly over years of matches, plus weapon-times-liability products. Leaned on0.62

TabPFN leaned on this family more than any other, although in linear models it added nothing against the odds. That mismatch is the puzzle of this model: either TabPFN reads these noisy, correlated traits better, or it is picking up the timing leak. The audit flagged this family in particular: a weekly IOS solve can absorb the late rounds of a two-week event before an overlapping event starts (see the time rule in section 3).

The shared recipe (IOS: implied offensive and defensive skill)

Ratings
For each statistic, every earlier match says: the server's value (log-odds) ≈ μ + Oserver − Dreturner. All players' O and D are solved together by ridge regression (conjugate gradient on the sparse system), re-solved weekly from earlier weeks only, plus this event's earlier rounds. A player's D is their skill as a returner at holding that statistic down.
Tuning
Memory (half-life), ridge strength κ and weighting by serve points were tuned per statistic on 2013–2018, scored on forecasting the next match's statistic (not the winner).
Products
prod_s = OA × (−DB) − OB × (−DA): A's weapon against B's weakness, minus the reverse. A linear model of A−B differences cannot form these on its own.

These exist so TabPFN could find interactions (e.g. serve traits matter more on grass). It found almost none: interactions explain about 2% of its correction to the price, and this family carries none of it.

What TabPFN actually did with the 54

Reduced to its straight-line part, TabPFN's correction to the price keeps +0.94 of its +1.57 gain (men, 2022–26). A separate curve per input explains 96% of the correction's variance; interactions add 2 points more. So the gain is mostly a better weighting of noisy, correlated inputs than a logistic regression manages, not hidden interactions. No free model reproduced it on the same inputs: linear +0.09, ridge +0.19, spline GAM +0.21, boosted trees +0.30, TabICL +0.08 (men, 2022–26, against the recalibrated close).

On average it moves the recalibrated price by 1.7 percentage points and changes the favourite in 610 of 23,819 test matches. In 2026 for men: 1.8 points on average, 42 favourite changes in 1,774 matches.

6 · Threads for our discussion

Where the process, not the sport, may be holding us back

  • We have tested "a better forecaster than the close", never "beat the close"

    Your friend's claim is closing-line value: getting a better price than the close. That needs a model better than the price when the bet is placed, usually earlier and softer, not better than the final price. Every study here benchmarks against the closing price, the hardest target there is. The Polymarket study already shows the close is beatable in tennis: 831 of 18,804 regular accounts clearly beat it, by +1.26¢ per $1 before fees (+0.93¢ after), with the most at Challenger and WTA 125 level (+1.75¢). We hold Polymarket's price history and every trade, so bet-time prices can be tested.

  • The leak repair threw away 91% of the history

    To fix the timing leak, the repair kept only matches with a provider-dated match day: 78,714 of 872,704. Challenger, ITF and qualifying history went, and the repaired no-odds model came out 14.4 milli-log-loss worse than the original on the same matches. Nobody has measured how much of that was leak and how much was lost history. Two ways to remove the leak while keeping the history: real match days for the lower levels (already on disk in part: Flashscore start times for ATP and Challenger singles to April 2025, more ITF and WTA from its head-to-head crawl, api-tennis dates for 2021–24), or a rule that releases a lower-level result only after its event's last possible day.

  • The one 2026 winner has not been re-run cleanly

    The study's own next step: rebuild the long-memory IOS profile on dated history, then refit TabPFN-3.5-Thinking with the same design on repaired features. If the men's gain survives, it is the first lead worth a betting-grade test. If it collapses, the leak explained it.

  • We test where the market is sharpest

    Public closing odds exist only for the main tour, so every closing-line test ran on the most efficient tennis markets. Polymarket also lists Challenger, WTA 125 and ITF matches, where the sharp accounts earn the most. Pinnacle's odds also stop in January 2026, so our 2026 benchmark is the average of books, which scored 0.7 milli-log-loss worse than Pinnacle.

7 · Glossary

Tennis and statistics terms used on this page

Open the glossary
Serve, return
Each point starts with one player serving; the other returns. Serving is an advantage: men win about 63% of serve points on hard courts.
Game, set, match
Points make games (first to 4, by 2), games make sets (first to 6, by 2, or a tiebreak at 6–6), sets make the match (best of 3, or best of 5 in men's Grand Slams).
Hold, break
Winning your own service game is a hold; winning the opponent's is a break.
Break point
A point that would win the game for the returner. The server saves it; the returner converts it.
Ace, double fault
An ace is a serve the returner never touches. A double fault is two missed serves, a lost point.
First and second serve
A missed first serve gets a second try. Players hit the first hard and the second safe, so second-serve points are where returners attack.
Tiebreak
A first-to-7-points game that settles a set at 6–6. Mostly luck from one match to the next.
Surface
Hard (medium), clay (slow, high bounce, long rallies), grass (fast, low bounce, serve-dominated). Carpet was indoor and is gone.
Levels
Grand Slams (4 a year), Tour Finals, 1000s, 500s, 250s form the main tour. Below it: Challenger (men) / WTA 125 (women), then ITF. Qualifying rounds feed the main draws.
Retirement, walkover
A player who stops mid-match retires; one who never starts gives a walkover. Here the player who advanced is counted as the winner after a retirement; walkovers are excluded.
Log loss, milli-log-loss
−ln(probability given to the winner), averaged. Coin 0.693. A milli-log-loss is 0.001. Positive gains on this page mean better.
The ladder
(coin's log loss − model's) / (coin's − the close's). 0% is a coin, 100% is the closing line.
De-vig, overround
Implied probabilities from odds sum above 1 (the bookmaker's margin). Dividing by the sum removes it.
Recalibrated close
The close passed through a one-slope logistic fit on training seasons. It is a slightly stronger benchmark than the raw close.
Given Elo, given odds
How much adding a feature to surface Elo, or to the closing odds, improves the forecast, fitted season by season on earlier seasons only.
Paired 95% interval
Uncertainty of a gain computed match by match on the same matches, grouped into four-week blocks of tournament dates so that related matches are not counted as independent.
Half-life
How long until an old match counts half as much. Two weeks means last month's matches barely count.
Shrinkage, prior
Pulling an average toward the tour norm (or zero) by a number of pseudo-matches, so a player with three matches is not judged on three matches.
Ridge
A penalty that pulls coefficients or ratings toward zero to stop them chasing noise.
iid point model
Treats every point as an independent coin with the server's win chance, and computes exactly the chance of winning the match from it.
Leak
Information from after a match reaching a feature for that match. Here: late rounds of a two-week event reaching another event that started during it.
