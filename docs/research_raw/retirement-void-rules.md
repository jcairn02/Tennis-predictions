# Research archive: retirement-void-rules

*Raw structured output from the 2026-06-10 multi-agent research run. Facts below are as-reported by research agents; entries in the Verifications section were independently adversarially checked.*

## Facts

### Industry rule taxonomy (high)
Tennis retirement settlement rules fall into standard classes used across the industry: ball-served = action (bet stands once any point is played; advancing player wins), one-set-completed = action (bet stands only if at least one full set was completed before retirement), and match-completed = void (bet refunded unless the full match finishes or the market outcome was already unconditionally determined); some books add a disqualification exception that settles to the advancing player. Outplayed's May 18, 2026-updated catalog confirms these classes are current.

Source: https://outplayed.com/blog/tennis-retirement-rules

### Pinnacle moneyline retirement rule (high) **[decision-critical]**
Pinnacle's official betting rules (fetched directly June 10, 2026) state: "If a competitor retires or is disqualified bets on the Match Money Line market will have action as long as one Set has been completed, otherwise these bets will be voided." So Pinnacle is a one-set-completed = action book for the match winner market, not a void-on-retirement book.

Source: https://www.pinnacle.com/en/future/betting-rules

### Pinnacle other markets and postponement (high) **[decision-critical]**
Pinnacle's official rules state that on retirement or disqualification "Bets on other markets will be deemed void unless the period was played to completion" (handicaps, totals, set bets void regardless of score), and "All bets on a match have action as long as the match is completed within seven days of when it was originally scheduled" with bets standing regardless of venue or surface change; a walkover (match never completed) therefore voids match bets.

Source: https://www.pinnacle.com/en/future/betting-rules

### BetOnline moneyline retirement rule (high) **[decision-critical]**
BetOnline's official tennis rules (help center article; page bot-blocked but text extracted via search index in two independent queries) state: "One full set must be completed for money line wagers to have action," while the full match must be completed for any spread or total wagers (game and set spreads/totals) to have action. BetOnline is a one-set book for moneyline as of June 2026.

Source: https://help.betonline.ag/en/articles/185194-tennis-rules

### BetOnline sister-site corroboration (high)
Sportsbetting.ag (same BetOnline group) publishes the identical rule — "One full set must be completed for moneyline wagers to have action" with full-match requirement for spreads/totals — corroborating the BetOnline group rule text.

Source: https://help.sportsbetting.ag/en/articles/201103-tennis-rules

### Bookmaker.eu moneyline retirement rule (high) **[decision-critical]**
Bookmaker.eu's official rules page (fetched directly June 10, 2026) states for tennis: "Matchup must be fully completed without player's withdrawal, incapacity or disqualification for bets to stand" — i.e., full-match-required: a retirement at any score voids match moneyline bets (stake refunded). This contradicts the old CRIS-era folklore that Bookmaker was a ball-served book.

Source: https://www.bookmaker.eu/rules

### Bookmaker.eu disqualification exception (medium) **[decision-critical]**
BookMaker's dedicated help article 'Retirements or Disqualifications' states that in the event of a match starting but not being completed all bets are void UNLESS (after the start of the match) a player is disqualified, in which case the player progressing to the next round or awarded the victory is deemed the winner for settlement purposes — so at Bookmaker.eu retirement = void but disqualification = action. Note the main rules page wording ('without...disqualification') superficially conflicts; the dedicated article is the more specific rule.

Source: https://get.bookmaker.help/hc/en-us/articles/360008925977-Retirements-or-Disqualifications

### Sportsbet.io retirement rule (high) **[decision-critical]**
Sportsbet.io's official tennis rules state that once the first ball has been served, all match bets are VOIDED on a retirement of a player or pair unless the outcome of the bet has already been concluded (bets whose outcome was already unconditionally determined — e.g., the progressing player in a market already decided — are settled as winners); Yolo Group's own sportsbet.jp guide mirror confirms: "In the event of a player's retirement from the tennis match, only undecided bets will be void." So Sportsbet.io is effectively a void-on-retirement (full-match) book for the match-winner market, the Betradar-standard rule.

### Sportsbet.io walkover rule (high) **[decision-critical]**

### Polymarket retirement rule (high) **[decision-critical]**
Polymarket's live tennis match-market rules template (read June 10, 2026 from the official Gamma API description of an open June 11, 2026 ITF match market, Burel vs Chiesa) states: "If the match begins but is not completed, and one player advances due to the opponent's retirement, default, or disqualification, this market will resolve to the player who advances." This is the most action-leaning rule of all venues surveyed — equivalent to ball-served = action with the advancing player paid in full, even on a first-game retirement.

Source: https://gamma-api.polymarket.com/events?tag_slug=tennis&closed=false&order=id&ascending=false&limit=5

### Polymarket walkover/cancellation rule (high) **[decision-critical]**
The same Polymarket tennis market template states: "If the match ends in a walkover (player withdraws before the start and the other advances automatically), this market will resolve to 50-50," and if the match is canceled, ends in a tie, or is delayed beyond 7 days without a winner, it also resolves 50-50. A 50-50 resolution pays $0.50 per share to BOTH sides — it is NOT a refund: a walkover loses money for anyone holding the favorite above 50c and profits anyone holding the underdog below 50c, a settlement behavior unique among the 12 venues.

### Walkover handling across conventional books (high) **[decision-critical]**
A walkover (withdrawal before any point is played) voids match-winner bets with stakes refunded at effectively all conventional sportsbooks and the Betfair Exchange — confirmed explicitly for Sportsbet.io, Stake ('walkover' named in the void rule), and implied by completion requirements at Pinnacle/BetOnline/Cloudbet/SX/Bookmaker — with Polymarket the sole exception (50-50 resolution, a real P&L event).

Source: https://www.rulesofsport.com/betting/tennis/what-are-the-rules-on-retirements-when-betting-on-tennis/

### Retirement base rate 2025-2026 (high)
4.8% of completed-bracket ATP-level matches in the 2025 season ended in mid-match retirement — the highest rate since the ATP Tour's creation in 1990 (previous peaks 4.3% in 2008, 2009, 2014) — so the settlement-rule differences between books are economically material at roughly a 1-in-20 match frequency (December 8, 2025 report).

Source: https://www.puntodebreak.com/en/2025/12/08/the-atp-season-in-2025-ends-with-record-of-matches-resolved-by-retirements

### Retirement timing distribution (medium)
Peer-reviewed analysis of ATP/WTA tour events (European Journal of Sport Science, 2024) found Grand Slam men's matches historically end in retirement at about a 2.69% rate, and recent Grand Slam data shows the second set is the most common retirement point (~39% of retirements) — implying a substantial minority of retirements happen BEFORE one completed set, the scenario where one-set books void but Polymarket still settles.

Source: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ejsc.12177

### EV math: rules change effective price (high) **[decision-critical]**
Identical quoted odds are different EV under different settlement rules. Decompose outcomes for a bet on player A at decimal odds d: with completion probability (1−r), let p_c = P(A wins | completed); with retirement probability r, let q = P(A is the advancing player | retirement), and split r into r_1 (retirement before 1 completed set) and r_2 (after ≥1 set). Then EV(full-match/void book: Stake, Bookmaker.eu, Sportsbet.io, bet365 base) = (1−r)(p_c·d − 1); EV(one-set book: Pinnacle, BetOnline, Cloudbet, SX, Bet105, Betfair Exchange) = (1−r)(p_c·d − 1) + r_2(q·d − 1); EV(advancing-player venue: Polymarket) = (1−r)(p_c·d − 1) + r(q·d − 1). The action terms are positive exactly when q·d > 1, so the same line is worth more at action books for the side LESS likely to retire and more at void books for the side MORE likely to retire.

Source: https://www.pinnacle.com/en/future/betting-rules

### Cross-book rule arbitrage (high) **[decision-critical]**
Because the sharp price discovery venues (Pinnacle one-set; Polymarket full-action) settle retirements as action, the market price of a player with injury/withdrawal rumors embeds a retirement-risk discount; backing that injury-doubt player at a void-on-retirement book (Stake, Bookmaker.eu, Sportsbet.io, or bet365 retail) at approximately the same price captures the discount without bearing the retirement scenario (refund instead of loss), while backing the healthy opponent is worth more at action venues (Polymarket pays even on a first-game retirement; one-set books pay after one set). This 'bet injured players where bets void' angle is a long-documented practice among professional arbitrage/value bettors.

Source: https://arbusers.com/betting-on-injured-tennis-players-t10591/

### Kelly sizing under void rules (high) **[decision-critical]**
Void/refund outcomes are pushes that change optimal stake sizing: with push probability π (retirement-void scenarios), win probability w and loss probability l = 1−w−π, the Kelly-optimal fraction of bankroll is the standard two-outcome formula applied to the renormalized conditional probabilities w/(1−π) and l/(1−π), scaled by (1−π) exposure — at a void book an injury-doubt position has lower effective variance and lower stake-at-risk than the same nominal stake at an action book, so per-venue settlement rules must enter the bet-sizing layer, not just the EV layer.

Source: https://www.pinnacle.com/en/future/betting-rules

### Model training: retired matches (high)
Because the 12 venues settle the same retired match as a win (Polymarket; one-set books after set 1), a void (full-match books; one-set books before set 1), or even a 50-50 cash event (Polymarket walkovers), a single 'winner' label for retired matches is settlement-rule-dependent and contaminated by injury information rather than skill: the model should (a) train the win-probability head on completed matches only (or duplicate-label retirements with a completion-conditional target), (b) maintain a separate per-player retirement/withdrawal hazard estimate (recent retirements, MTOs, age — Tennis Abstract documents retirement non-randomness) used to convert model win probability into venue-specific EV via the rule formulas, and (c) settle every historical bet in backtests and CLV tracking using the actual rule of the venue the bet would have been placed at, since applying one settlement convention misstates ROI by roughly the 4.8% retirement rate times the odds-weighted rule difference.

Source: https://www.tennisabstract.com/blog/category/withdrawals-and-retirements/

### Bookmaker.eu walkover oddity (low) **[decision-critical]**
Bookmaker.eu's rules page contains the sentence "If a player withdraws before participating, then all bets on that player are settled as a loss" — almost certainly tournament-futures context (withdrawal before the tournament makes outright bets losers), not match-walkover settlement, but the page's structure makes the scope ambiguous; worth confirming with support before placing match bets there around withdrawal rumors, since a literal match-bet reading would be punitive.

Source: https://www.bookmaker.eu/rules
