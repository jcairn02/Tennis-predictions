# Research index

Research records evidence and unresolved questions; it does not authorize implementation or set a roadmap.

The [tennis data and Polymarket audit](tennis-data-audit/README.md) covers September 10, 2025 through September 10, 2026: an observed market catalog, public and commercial/private source research, direct payload checks, conservative market/result joins, and price-history probes. Start with the synthesis, then the [source register](tennis-data-audit/source-register.md) and [evidence index](../../data/studies/tennis_data_audit/README.md). These are findings and limitations, not a source selection or implementation plan.

[Granular data leads](granular-data-leads/README.md) (24 September 2026) ranks, by confidence, where shot-by-shot, tracking and point-by-point data the dump does not hold can be found, including Grand Slam feeds, official tour sites, public tracking caches and paid feeds. It compares, field by field, the per-point statistics each source would add. Evidence is in [data/studies/granular_data_leads/](../../data/studies/granular_data_leads/). It lists options, not decisions. Its [access routes](granular-data-leads/access-routes.md) follow-up checks whether the Slam feeds and the WTA API are public without a scraping ban, or sold by anyone. A second follow-up, [TDI and open data](granular-data-leads/access-routes-tdi-and-open-data.md), does the same for TDI Tennis Results and four open datasets.

[Gap sources](gap-sources/README.md) (29 September 2026) checks, on random samples of the data map's own gap matches, whether the sources each gap card names under "Could come from" hold the missing data, and proposes a final list per gap. It adds sources found on the way (the WTA website's match records, the ITF's live-data API, TennisLive's event listings) and records which suggestions failed. Evidence is in [data/studies/gap_sources/](../../data/studies/gap_sources/README.md). It lists options, not decisions.

[Rating systems for tennis](rating-systems/README.md) (2 October 2026) collects the published evidence on Glicko, Glicko-2, TrueSkill, TrueSkill Through Time and the other alternatives to Elo for predicting tennis matches. No peer-reviewed tennis study compares Glicko-2 with Elo. Glicko-1 and TrueSkill tie Elo where tested. Smoothing systems gain about 0.011 nats per match, but only against a constant-K Elo. A surface model is the most consistent extension. No rating has been shown to beat the betting market. The project's own test is the study [rating systems against Elo](../studies/rating_systems/README.md). It lists evidence, not decisions.

[Anchoring on the market](market-anchoring/README.md) (5 October 2026) collects the evidence behind a professional bettor's advice. It covers López de Prado's meta-labeling, ways of putting the odds inside a model (offset and residual models, Benter's second stage, encompassing tests, decorrelation, Black–Litterman), the calibration of tennis prices, surface Elo, and the status of Jeff Sackmann's data: his results repositories have returned 404 since June 2026, and TML continues the format. No sports-betting use of meta-labeling was found, and against sharp tennis closing prices combining a model with the odds has not helped. The project's own test is the study [anchor on the odds, nudge with features](../studies/market_anchoring/README.md). It lists evidence, not decisions.

[Match timing](match-timing/README.md) (5 October 2026) covers four questions for 2010–2026: when each round is played at every level, how late schedules slip, where lower-level match days can be found and under what terms, and which clock each source's dates follow. Its 455 rules, each with a source, are in `data/studies/match_timing/schedule_rules.csv`. The project uses them in the study [match timing](../studies/match_timing/README.md). It lists evidence, not decisions.

[BetOnline tennis lines](betonline-lines/README.md) (6 October 2026) looks for BetOnline's tennis opening and closing prices for 2024–2026, free first. OddsTrader's site serves them free, with every move timestamped, for about 97% of tour-level singles (qualifying included, no Challenger or ITF). Its terms allow personal use but say automated access may need written permission. The Odds API sells 5-minute snapshots that show BetOnline tennis in 2026; its tournaments reach at most 48% of 2024–26 main-tour matches, and BetOnline's presence there in 2024–25 is unverified. OddsPapi is free from January 2026, but whether it carries BetOnline tennis is unverified. Nothing else checked covers 2024–25. On the owner's [decision](../decisions/2026-10-06-oddstrader-betonline-lines.md), OddsTrader's lines were pulled on 7 October: 22,203 of the 22,854 singles played in 2024–2026 have BetOnline's opening and last match-winner line, 21,469 with a clean close. It lists options, not decisions.

[Serve speed sources](serve-speed-sources/README.md) (7 October 2026) measures how much first-serve speed the project holds for the men's serve-speed-change feature, and where the rest could come from. Findings:
* **Held today:** the feature exists for 9% of the men's 2025–26 holdout.
* **Biggest free gain:** the public GitHub dataset `msolonskyi/ManTennisData` publishes the ATP Stats Centre's per-match speed averages as CSV, every week, from October 2021. It would lift that to 49%.
* **Encrypted only:** AO 2025, RG 2025 and RG 2026 exist only in the Slams' encrypted Infosys files.
* **No free source:** WTA tour speeds in any year, and ATP 2015–20.
* **Paid:** no vendor publicly sells a non-Slam speed archive. The holders are TDI, SMT/IDS, Hawk-Eye, the WTA with Stats Perform, and the Slams. Contacts and a draft inquiry are included.

It lists options, not decisions.

[Ideas for point-by-point and shot-by-shot tests](point-shot-ideas/README.md) (started 8 October 2026) lists untested ideas waiting for better data. Each entry gives where the idea came from, what data it needs and what the project already holds. The first is physical condition from the rally, measured against the player's own normal: groundstroke speed, running per point, rally length and errors. Ball-tracking files for about 5,800 matches are on disk and unused.

The [inherited one-shot output](legacy-one-shot/INDEX.md) is kept separately as unapproved early research. Its source inventory is not exhaustive and its verification labels are not fresh validation.
