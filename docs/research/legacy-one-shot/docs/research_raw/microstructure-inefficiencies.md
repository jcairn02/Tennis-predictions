# Research archive: microstructure-inefficiencies

*Raw structured output from the 2026-06-10 multi-agent research run. Facts below are as-reported by research agents; entries in the Verifications section were independently adversarially checked.*

## Facts

### Line origination - who posts first (high)
Sharp market-making sportsbooks (Pinnacle, BookMaker/CRIS, Circa) post opening lines first with reduced limits to attract informed action for price discovery; soft retail books release later and largely mirror the sharp books' numbers, making the market a 'follow the leader' structure.

Source: https://unabated.com/articles/who-sets-the-sports-betting-line-market-makers

### Line origination - tennis-specific leaders (medium) **[decision-critical]**
For tennis specifically, practitioner guidance (betstamp sharp-betting guide) identifies Pinnacle and the Betfair exchange as the books whose lines lead the tennis market, with other books lagging 'by a few minutes or more, especially in tennis markets'; opening odds are posted 'often many hours (or even days, for big tournaments) before a match' and are based on the bookmaker's model with minimal betting input.

Source: https://betstamp.com/education/tennis-betting-strategy-guide

### Line origination - limit ladder (medium) **[decision-critical]**
Pinnacle opens markets at roughly 25% of their maximum limit and ramps limits toward full size near start time (illustrative ladder for a big soccer match: ~GBP 2,000 at open, 5,000 day before, 20,000 on match day, 50,000 an hour out), because the book grows confident in the price as informed action arrives; openers on low-tier tennis (Challenger) may accept only a few hundred dollars.

Source: https://www.pinnacleoddsdropper.com/guides/market-limits-and-vigs-what-are-they-and-why-are-they-important

### Challenger/ITF market limits (low) **[decision-critical]**
A Sportsbook Review forum thread on high-limit ITF tennis betting reported Pinnacle ITF match limits of roughly $447 Canadian per bet (older anecdote, not an official published figure); Pinnacle does not publish tier-by-tier tennis limits, and practitioner consensus is that ITF/Challenger limits are an order of magnitude below ATP/WTA main-tour limits.

Source: https://www.sportsbookreview.com/forum/players-talk/3276936-high-limits-bets-itf-tennis.html

### Open-vs-close efficiency (tennis) (high)
Sports Trading Network analyzed Pinnacle opening and closing odds for 68,361 ATP/WTA/Challenger/ITF matches (136,722 prices, May 12 2015 - July 19 2019) and found the opening-to-fair-closing price ratio predicts profitability with a trend-line gradient of 0.825 versus 1.0 for perfect efficiency, i.e., closing prices are more efficient than openers but prices 'do not fully converge to price equilibrium' by close.

Source: https://www.sportstradingnetwork.com/article/how-efficient-is-the-tennis-betting-market/

### Open-vs-close calibration / FLB (tennis) (high) **[decision-critical]**
In the same 68,361-match Pinnacle tennis dataset (2015-2019), selections with 90% implied probability won 89% of the time (ratio 0.989) while selections with 10% implied probability won only 6% of the time (ratio 0.60) — a favorite-longshot bias the author characterizes as 'fairly weak' at the favorite end but severe at the longshot end (a 10.0-odds tennis dog priced at fair 10% winning 6% implies roughly -40% ROI before margin).

Source: https://www.sportstradingnetwork.com/article/how-efficient-is-the-tennis-betting-market/

### Margin-removal method for devigging tennis odds (high) **[decision-critical]**
Sports Trading Network's tennis study found that removing Pinnacle's margin with a logarithmic (odds-proportional) function yielded +0.47% versus break-even expectation across the dataset, while assuming equal margin split across both sides yielded -2.73%, i.e., odds-proportional devigging is materially more accurate than equal-split for tennis two-way markets.

Source: https://www.sportstradingnetwork.com/article/how-efficient-is-the-tennis-betting-market/

### CLV vs Pinnacle close - skill benchmark (high)
Pinnacle's own betting-resources position (and its former Head of Trading Marco Blume) is that consistently beating Pinnacle's closing line is the most reliable indicator of long-term positive expected value, because Pinnacle's low-margin high-limit close is regarded as the most efficient price in the market; this is the standard professional CLV benchmark.

Source: https://www.pinnacle.com/betting-resources/en/betting-strategy/using-the-closing-line-to-test-your-skill-in-betting/7e6jwjm5ykejuwkq

### CLV caveat - winning without beating the close (tennis) (high)
A Sports Trading Network case study of ~3,000 ATP bets from tipster 'Nishikori' found +8.9% actual ROI at bet odds while devigged Pinnacle closing lines implied only -0.2% expected ROI (and +5.8% had bets been placed at close), and the top CLV quartile (+9.2% expected) actually yielded +19.9% — evidence that Pinnacle tennis closing prices are efficient only on average and CLV can understate a genuinely sharp tennis bettor's realized edge.

Source: https://www.sportstradingnetwork.com/article/do-pinnacle-closing-prices-in-tennis-tell-the-full-story-can-you-win-in-the-long-run-without-beating-them/

### Bookmaker consensus as accuracy ceiling (high)
Kovalchik (2016, Journal of Quantitative Analysis in Sports) tested 11 published tennis forecasting models on 2,395 ATP singles matches from the 2014 season and found the bookmaker consensus model was the most accurate (~70% match-winner accuracy), with the best statistical model (FiveThirtyEight-style Elo with career data) still 3-7% worse than bookmakers on prediction performance metrics.

Source: https://vuir.vu.edu.au/34652/1/jqas-2015-0059.pdf

### In-play share of tennis handle (high) **[decision-critical]**
Entain's 2025 trend report (published ~January 29, 2025) states that around 90% of all tennis bets placed with Entain brands are in-play — the highest in-play share of any major sport (football ~60-65%) — that tennis has outperformed American football in US in-play volume for three consecutive years, that 60% of tennis bets are on men's matches, and that match winner, current-game winner and set winner account for nearly 85% of in-play tennis wagers.

Source: https://www.gamblingnews.com/news/entain-tennis-bets-second-most-popular-in-the-us-and-the-uk/

### In-play share - industry corroboration (medium)
The 2024 IBIA/H2 Gambling Capital report 'The Availability of Sports Betting Products' states leading global operators averaged 66% of betting turnover from in-play in 2023 (58% of gross win), that US soccer generates ~70% of handle in-play with tennis 'much higher than this', and projects the regulated global tennis betting market at ~$4.4bn GGR in 2024 growing to over $6bn by 2028.

Source: https://h2gc.com/site/assets/News%20Stories/IBIA-Report-2024_FINAL-TEXT_SINGLE_AW.pdf

### Favorite-longshot bias - ATP magnitude (medium) **[decision-critical]**
A Pinnacle betting-resources analysis of ~39,886 ATP main-draw matches (2010 onward, Pinnacle closing odds) found blanket-backing favorites returned about -2.0% ROI versus about -5.6% for underdogs, with ROI turning sharply negative from decimal odds ~6.0 upward and the highest-priced longshot buckets showing some of the worst long-term returns in the dataset.

Source: https://www.pinnacle.com/betting-resources/en/educational/the-favorite-longshot-bias-in-atp-tennis-what-40000-matches-reveal

### Favorite-longshot bias - where it is strongest (high)
Lahvička (2014, Applied Economics Letters 21(2):90-92), using ~45,000 professional tennis matches, found the favorite-longshot bias is much stronger in matches between lower-ranked players, in later-round matches, and in high-profile tournaments, and concluded the pattern is consistent with bookmakers protecting themselves against informed insiders and fast-reacting bettors rather than pure bettor risk-love — implying wider effective margins on longshots exactly where insider (injury/tanking) information risk is highest.

Source: https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/47905/

### Favorite-longshot bias - exchanges (medium)
Abinzano, Muga & Santamaria found the favorite-longshot bias also exists on tennis betting exchanges (Betfair), with mispricing positively associated with trading volume and market uncertainty and negatively associated with institutional-bettor presence — so switching from bookmaker to exchange does not eliminate tennis FLB.

Source: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1527002517731875

### ITIA alerts 2021-2023 (annual) (high) **[decision-critical]**
ITIA match alerts totalled 113 in 2021, 109 in 2022, and 101 in 2023; in 2023 there were zero alerts at any Grand Slam and zero at ATP/WTA 500 or 1000 level events — alerts are overwhelmingly concentrated at Challenger, WTA 125 and ITF World Tennis Tour levels.

Source: https://www.tennis.com/news/articles/number-of-suspicious-tennis-matches-decreased-2023-there-were-none-grand-slam-tournaments-itia-review

### ITIA alerts 2024 (quarterly sum + tier mix) (high) **[decision-critical]**
ITIA quarterly updates for 2024 report 25 (Q1), 19 (Q2), 28 (Q3) and 23 (Q4) match alerts — 95 for the year; the Q4 2024 breakdown was entirely sub-tour-level: ATP Challenger 125/100/75/50 (2/1/3/1), WTA 125 (2), ITF men's M15/M25 (5/2), ITF women's W15/W35 (2/5), with zero at Grand Slam, ATP Tour or WTA main-tour level.

Source: https://www.itia.tennis/news/itia-news/itia-quarterly-update-q4-2024/

### ITIA alerts 2025 (quarterly sum + tier mix) (high) **[decision-critical]**
ITIA match alerts in 2025 totalled 68 (Q1: 9, Q2: 10, Q3: 26, Q4: 23); Q3 2025 included exactly one main-tour-level alert (US Open) with the other 25 at Challenger 50/75/100/125 and ITF M15/M25, and Q4 2025's 23 alerts were 100% Challenger/ITF/WTT level — confirming the multi-year pattern that integrity risk in tennis betting is concentrated almost entirely below the ATP/WTA main tours.

Source: https://www.itia.tennis/news/itia-news/itia-quarterly-update-quarter-3-2025/

### ITIA alerts Q1 2026 (high) **[decision-critical]**
In Q1 2026 the ITIA received 21 match alerts: 5 at ATP Challenger level (125: 1, 75: 3, 50: 1), 1 at WTA 250 level, and 15 at ITF World Tennis Tour level (M25: 2, M15: 2, W75: 4, W50: 4, W35: 1, W15: 2) — again ~95% below the main tours (data published April 28, 2026).

Source: https://www.itia.tennis/news/itia-news/itia-quarterly-update-q1-2026/

### Alert rate denominator (high)
A Tennis Integrity Unit briefing note contextualizes alert counts: 48 alerts in one quarter against 24,110 professional matches played worldwide in that period, i.e., roughly 0.2% of matches generate a suspicious-betting alert, and the ITIA stresses an alert alone is not evidence of fixing (legitimate causes include incorrect odds-setting, well-informed betting, and player fitness/fatigue).

Source: https://www.itia.tennis/news/itia-news/tennis-integrity-unit-briefing-note-april-2016/

### IBIA (operator-side) tennis alerts (high)
The International Betting Integrity Association (operator-side body including Pinnacle-class members) reported 219 total suspicious-betting alerts across all sports in 2024 with tennis second at 58 (football 75), and 300 total in 2025 with tennis again second at 74 alerts — tennis is consistently a top-2 sport for operator-reported suspicious betting.

Source: https://europeangaming.eu/portal/latest-news/2025/02/13/176312/ibia-publishes-2024-sports-betting-integrity-report-219-alerts-reported/

### ITF tier share of betting markets (high)
The 2018 Independent Review of Integrity in Tennis found that low-value ITF events accounted for about 68% of all tennis matches on which betting markets were available (2017 figures), and recommended discontinuing official data supply for the lowest ITF tiers — the structural reason integrity risk and thin liquidity concentrate at the bottom of the pyramid.

Source: https://www.atptour.com/-/media/files/independent-review-of-integrity-in-tennis-document-2018.pdf

### Challenger market information environment (medium)
Pinnacle's own Challenger-tennis article (Dan Weston) notes that at Challenger level 'available information - such as injury news - can be sketchy' and modest prize money, humble venues and small crowds introduce motivational/situational factors, which it frames as a genuine edge opportunity for bettors who do the research; Pinnacle offers Challenger markets but with materially lower limits than tour-level.

Source: https://pinnacle.com/en/betting-articles/tennis/the-challenge-of-betting-challenger-tennis/3muj3jsdsgtg88us

### WTA vs ATP pricing efficiency - intransitivity (high)
A graph-neural-network study (arXiv 2510.20454, Oct 2025) of 16,663 ATP and 16,447 WTA matches (Jan 2014 - Jun 2025) using Pinnacle odds found women's tennis exhibits 11.5% more matchup intransitivity than men's (3.02 vs 2.71), that bookmaker odds remain essentially transitive (failing to price matchup-specific dynamics), and that targeting high-intransitivity matches (threshold >2.55) produced +3.26% ROI with Kelly staking over 1,903 bets against Pinnacle prices.

Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2510.20454v1

### WTA inefficiency claims - cautionary correction (high)
Ramirez, Reade & Singleton's 'Betting on a buzz' (International Journal of Forecasting 2023) claimed Wikipedia page-view 'buzz' predicted bookmaker mispricing with 17-29% ROI over 5,000+ WTA bets at Bet365, but the Clegg & Cartlidge correction study ('Not feeling the buzz', arXiv 2306.01740) showed most profits came from a single erroneously long-odds outlier bet (the 'Hercog' bet); after cleaning, only one strategy variant stayed profitable in-sample and post-2020 out-of-sample profits disappeared entirely — published WTA-inefficiency ROI claims should be treated skeptically.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01740

### WTA predictability context (medium)
Tennis Abstract (Jeff Sackmann) documented that the higher-ranked WTA player won only 62.4% of matches through Rome 2017 — the worst rate in 28 years versus a 67.9% historical average since 1990 — i.e., women's tennis outcomes are structurally noisier relative to rankings, which raises model variance even if betting-market prices remain calibrated.

Source: https://www.tennisabstract.com/blog/2017/05/28/the-steadily-less-predictable-wta/

### Fatigue - final-set-tiebreak study (medium)
Dan Weston's Pinnacle study of top-70 ATP players (June 2013 - June 2014, 88 matches, Pinnacle closing prices) found players who had just won a final-set tiebreak (proxy for an extra-long ~32-game match vs ~23-game average) won their next match only 42.05% of the time, but level-stakes backing of them returned only -2.98% — i.e., the fatigue effect is real but already approximately priced into the closing line, with no exploitable edge at level stakes in this sample.

Source: https://www.pinnacle.com/en/betting-articles/tennis/final-set-tiebreak-betting/a4s2rrzjmyvw6t33

### Fatigue - ATP 250 winner's curse (practitioner, weak) (low)
A Tennisnerd practitioner article claims roughly 30-40% of ATP 250 title winners lose their opening match the following week (Sunday final then immediate travel), and that markets often still price them as confident favorites, but the author explicitly concedes these are 'observed trends, not rigidly verified statistics' with no ROI calculation, sample size, or defined period — anecdotal only.

Source: https://www.tennisnerd.net/articles/the-atp-250-winners-curse-logistics-fatigue-and-betting-roi/65587

### Retirement settlement rule - Pinnacle (verified) (high) **[decision-critical]**
Pinnacle's published tennis rules state: 'If a competitor retires or is disqualified bets on the Match Money Line market will have action as long as one Set has been completed, otherwise these bets will be voided', other markets are void unless the relevant period was completed before retirement, and walkovers/forfeits void all bets — so at Pinnacle a moneyline bet wins if the backed player advances via opponent retirement after one completed set.

Source: https://www.pinnacle.com/en/future/betting-rules

### Retirement settlement rules vary by book (high) **[decision-critical]**
Tennis retirement settlement rules across bookmakers fall into four distinct categories — 'one ball served' (bet stands once play begins, e.g., common at US books), 'one set completed', 'two sets completed', and 'full match completed' — meaning identical bets on the same retirement-ended match can win, lose, or void depending on the book, a material settlement-EV difference for any tennis model.

Source: https://www.oddsmonkey.com/blog/matched-betting/tennis-betting-retirement-rules/

### Retirement frequency by tier (high) **[decision-critical]**
A 2024 peer-reviewed study of second/third-tier professional tennis found retirements end 2.94% of ATP Challenger matches, 3.44% of ITF men's World Tennis Tour matches, 2.94% of WTA 125 matches and 2.73% of ITF women's matches (ATP main draws run ~2.1%), with retirement incidence trending upward over time — at lower tiers roughly 1 in 30 bets faces retirement settlement risk, which interacts directly with each book's retirement rule and with longshot pricing.

Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11146734/

### Injury news and line moves (medium)
No public quantitative study was found measuring whether tennis injury/withdrawal news is fully priced pre-match; the available evidence is indirect: the ITIA explicitly lists 'well-informed betting' and 'player fitness, fatigue or form' as legitimate causes of unusual line moves, and Lahvička's insider-protection explanation of the tennis FLB implies books defensively shade longshot prices because fitness information asymmetry is endemic, especially at lower tiers where injury news 'can be sketchy' (Pinnacle's own characterization).

Source: https://www.itia.tennis/news/itia-news/what-is-a-betting-alert/

### Exchange liquidity at lower tiers (low)
Practitioner analysis notes Betfair exchange prices closely track the sharpest bookmakers and lead price discovery only where matched volume is deep; for low-liquidity markets such as ATP Challenger tennis, a consensus of sharp bookmakers outperforms exchange prices as a fair-value baseline because exchange volume is too thin.

Source: https://www.sharkbetting.com/blog/sharp-books-explained

### Margin-removal method for devigging tennis odds — vote 1 — **confirmed**
Corrected/current fact: Confirmed with one correction to terminology: Joseph Buchdahl's tennis study (published via Pinnacle and republished on Sports Trading Network) found that devigging Pinnacle closing odds with the LOGARITHMIC (power) function gave a +0.47% yield when blindly betting every player to level stakes — closest to the break-even expectation — versus -2.73% when assuming the margin is split equally across both sides (-7.18% with margin left in), over 68,361 ATP/WTA/Challenger/ITF matches (136,722 prices), May 12 2015 – July 19 2019. So equal-split devigging does materially misdescribe Pinnacle's tennis margin, which is loaded disproportionately onto longer prices (favourite–longshot bias). CORRECTION: the claim's gloss 'logarithmic (odds-proportional)' conflates two distinct methods — in Buchdahl's own taxonomy the logarithmic/power function is NOT the 'margin proportional to odds' (MPTO) method; the article tested only the logarithmic function vs equal split, and the +0.47% figure belongs to the logarithmic method specifically. Implement the power/log devig, not MPTO, to reproduce it. Note also the data window ended July 2019; this is a fixed historical finding about Pinnacle's then-current margin distribution, not a guarantee about 2026 pricing.
