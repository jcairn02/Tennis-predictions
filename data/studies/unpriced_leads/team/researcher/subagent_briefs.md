## Lane L1: body, schedule and conditions

Topics, in order of priority:
1. **Fatigue, rest and scheduling**: previous-match duration or sets, consecutive days, short rest (less than ~20 hours), late finishes and the next day, night sessions and time of day (circadian effects on serve or match outcome), back-to-back events (e.g., Masters 1000 swings), season load and late-season fatigue; doubles played in the same event.
2. **Travel and jet lag**: time zones crossed, direction (eastward vs westward), days since arrival; evidence from tennis first, then from other individual sports, then team sports (MLB/NBA/NHL) only as mechanism.
3. **Heat, humidity, wind, altitude**: WBGT and heat policy studies (AO, US Open, ATP heat rule 2026), performance and retirement effects; wind and match variance (upsets?); altitude (Bogotá, Quito, Kitzbühel, Gstaad, Madrid ~650 m, Mexico City, Johannesburg) and serve dominance.
4. **Balls and court speed**: ball brand/type changes by event, ball-change injury claims, ITF Court Pace Rating / Court Pace Index by edition, effects on serve dominance and upsets; venue edition changes (e.g., Paris 2025).
5. **Retirements, injuries and returns**: retirement and walkover rates, predictors of retirement, the first matches back after injury or layoff (performance vs pre-layoff level), medical time-outs, protected rankings.
6. **Age and career curves**: peak age, rate of decline, whether peak age has shifted in the 2010s–2020s, age × fatigue/recovery, junior-to-pro trajectories, "rising young players under-priced/over-priced".

## Lane L2: scoring structure, luck and psychology

Topics, in order of priority:
1. **Break points, tiebreaks, deciding sets and "clutch"**: is over-performance a skill or luck? Year-to-year persistence, regression to the mean, Kovalchik & Ingram's "Hot heads, cool heads, and tacticians: measuring the mental game in tennis" (MIT Sloan 2016) — try hard to find it; any "pressure index" work.
2. **Conversion luck**: sets/games/matches won beyond what points won imply (Pythagorean or dominance-ratio-type expectations in tennis), and whether the residual regresses or predicts future results; margin-of-victory information (games or points won) beyond the win/loss result.
3. **Deviations from the iid point model**: serve clustering, point dependence, set dependence, score-state effects; any work since 2015; Jackson & Mosurski 1997 ("Heavy defeats in tennis") and similar classics not in the known list.
4. **Hot hand and momentum**: within a match and across matches/tournaments (title last week, winning streaks, comeback wins), and whether betting markets over- or under-react (pre-match and in-play).
5. **Gender differences under pressure**: Paserman (gender differences in performance in competitive environments, tennis), Cohen-Zada, Krumer, Rosenboim & Shapir 2017 (choking and gender), Krumer and co-authors' other tennis work, "first-mover" or psychological-advantage papers in tennis.
6. **Serving versus returning**: which predicts future results better (serve points won vs return points won, holds vs breaks), whether markets or rankings over-value serving; Sackmann or others on "return is undervalued".

## Lane L3: markets, incentives and the draw

Topics, in order of priority:
1. **Market efficiency of tennis closing lines, 2018–2026**: any study of Pinnacle/sharp closes, soft vs sharp books (e.g., recreational books keeping more bias), exchange vs bookmaker, women's vs men's market efficiency, lower tiers (Challenger, ITF) efficiency and integrity, opening-to-closing information.
2. **Favourite–longshot and other biases in tennis** published since 2019 (not in the known list): sentiment/popularity, nationality of bettors, recency or over-reaction to recent results (pre-match), reaction to injury news and withdrawals, retirement pricing and settlement rules.
3. **Incentives**: prize-money spreads and effort (Sunde 2009; Gilsdorf & Sukhatme 2008; Lallemand, Plasman & Rycx 2008; others), ranking-points incentives, effort before a Slam and letdown after a Slam or a title, dead rubbers/round robins, tanking, the superstar effect (playing against a top star), mandatory-event effects.
4. **Qualifiers, lucky losers, wild cards and entry type**: do qualifiers beat expectations (rhythm, conditions), and is it priced?
5. **Lower-level results**: how Challenger/ITF results translate to tour level; whether rankings or markets under-rate players arriving from lower levels; any "Challenger Elo" or level-translation work.
6. **Home advantage and crowds** beyond Koning 2011 and Gucciardi & Ruberti 2025: any test against betting odds; Davis Cup/team events; crowd absence (COVID) and betting.
