# Data map: measured coverage

The numbers behind the [data map](../../data_map.html). `src/eda/data_map/measure.py` writes them, and `src/eda/data_map/build.py` runs it and draws the page. The catalog it reads for dataset statuses is [data_map.yaml](../../data_map.yaml).

| File | What it holds | In git |
|---|---|---|
| `coverage_cells.csv` | One row per tour, tournament level and year, 2018 to the last complete week: matches, estimated missing matches, matches by best detail (shot, point, stats, result) counting everything and counting only data on disk, and matches by the status of their best detail | Yes |
| `coverage.json` | The same cells, plus each source's matches in each cell, how each source matched the list of matches, and the run's notes | Yes |
| `build_report.json` | The last build: catalog problems, unmapped folders, live progress of the downloads marked `downloading` | Yes |
| `matches.parquet` | Every match in the map with its level, best detail and the sources that hold it | No, rebuilt |
| `tml_redated.csv` | Tennis My Life's rows of misdated Challenger editions, each with the date and tournament name the map gave it and the file that dates it (Sackmann's twin, TennisLive's listing, or the rest of its event) | Yes |
| `_cache/` | Summaries of the slow sources (Infosys records, WTA point-log states, the WTA website's records, the Kaggle SofaScore copy) | No, rebuilt with `--refresh` (needed after a loader changes: the caches key on the input files only) |

## Method in brief

1. **The list of matches.** Built from the result files in order: Tennis My Life, Sackmann, the WTA's match lists, the Slam draws, the WTA website's records, api-tennis and TDI's listings. Singles only; walkovers are left out.
   * **Tennis My Life's misdated editions** (since 14 Oct 2026; `repair_tml_dates`). Tennis My Life files some Challenger editions under another edition's name and date (Irving 2018 as "Dallas", Biella 3-7 of 2021 as "Biella 1"), often months away, so the same match in Sackmann's files would join the list again. A Tennis My Life row whose own match Sackmann's files (else TennisLive's listings) hold more than 10 days and at most a year away first takes that date and name. Its own match means the same pair, round and games per set, in an event of the same id or sharing a name word. Rows with two such twins, or with one at their own date, stay as they are. On the build of 14 Oct this moved 1,841 rows and removed all 1,575 doubles of this kind that the [P6 data-issues study](../../../docs/studies/p6_data_issues/README.md) had listed. The gap downloads' frozen lists still name the old copies, so their placement (`placement_index`) also reads the old keys of the merged copies.
2. **Matching the other files.** Every other file is matched to the list by tour, the pair of surnames and the date (a window of 10 days before to 21 after, allowing for tournament-start versus match-day dating; 40 days either way for Grand Slam qualifying, which the 2021 Australian Open played in Doha and Dubai four weeks early). Two fallbacks follow: one surname agrees and the other names share a word (names cut short or reordered), or one player and the round agree in the same week (a changed surname). Both need the two players' first names to agree, which keeps sisters and namesakes apart (Naomi and Mari Osaka; the many players named Liu at the ITF events of one week). Before that test, about 6,400 Sackmann rows had been merged into other players' matches (8 Oct 2026). Infosys joins on the ATP's own tournament and player ids first. A row that matches nothing joins the list as a new match.
   * **Grand Slam rows are placed within their own edition** (since 16 Oct 2026; `Universe.link`, `slam_place`, at the owner's request), from every file: a row of level GS or GSQ, or of no level whose tournament name names a Slam (api-tennis gives its Slam rows none). Its edition (`slam_editions`) is its Slam's name, else the window from three weeks before the main draw's first day (six for the 2021 Australian Open's qualifying, played in Doha and Dubai) to 16 days after it. It is matched only to the Grand Slam matches of that edition and tour, by tiers: both players the same (`same_player`) with both first names agreeing or the same round; both the same, first names unread (surname-only lists, initials, surname-first forms: "Sherif Ahmed Abdelaziz", "C. J. Barranco", "H. Hsu Yu"); a surname spelt a letter apart, first names agreeing ("Wuerth" / "Wurth", "Görges" / "Goerges", "Sobolieva" / "Soboleva"); a changed surname in the same round ("Allertova" / "Satralova"). First names alone never place ("Valentin Royer v Marc Polmans" is not "Valentin Vacherot v Marc-Andrea Huesler"). A pair meets once in an edition, so the round and the draw only rank within a tier: files mislabel both (SofaScore's Kaggle copy files 342 qualifying matches of 2021-2024 as main draw; the Australian Open's archived draws call the qualifying's last round "Final"). A row no match fits joins the list; it never takes the pair's meeting at another event. A row of another known level never joins a Grand Slam match, and a Slam match a file lists twice under two spellings joins once. The gap downloads' frozen lists still name the copies these rows used to make, so their keys place as aliases (`placement_index`). On the build of 16 Oct (22:12 UTC): Grand Slam rows on other events' matches 145 → 0, other events' rows on Grand Slam matches 47 → 0; Grand Slam cells over their draw sizes 12 → 3 (44 → 9 matches over); Grand Slam matches 16,386 → 16,335; matches at other levels +17 (Challenger, ITF and tour qualifying matches the Slam matches had absorbed). The Australian Open's and Roland-Garros's Infosys match centres (`load_infosys_slams`, with full names) and Roland-Garros's own statistics (`load_rolandgarros`) place this way: all 1,410 and 3,748 rows on known matches, the qualifying matches the result files lacked (AO 2025: 1; RG 2021: 2; RG 2022: 1; RG 2026: 3) now made by the result files' own rows.
3. **Best detail.** Each match takes the richest detail any file gives it, and records whether that detail is on disk, downloading or assumed.
4. **Missing matches.** Estimated three ways: Grand Slam draw sizes (127 main-draw and 112 qualifying singles matches per tour per Slam); the rest of the current season after a level's files stop, from the same weeks of the season before; and a whole season where no file holds a level at all.
5. **What exists (drawn by the page).** The catalog's `universe` lists outside sources with the share of each tour, level and year they are measured or inferred to hold at each detail, or counts (the women's website-only charts), or files counted on disk (the encrypted ATP Stats Centre files, by file name only). Per cell the page takes, for each detail, the most any single source reaches, or adds a `disjoint` source's matches to ours. Paid feeds are listed, not counted.

Known limits: a player who changed surname and is not matched by round can be counted twice, as can one whose first name is spelled differently in a fallback (Dasha / Daria). At the Grand Slams (step 2) these remain on the build of 16 Oct: married names where one file gives no round (Laura Ioana Paar / Andrei, Storm Sanders / Hunter, Kateryna Kozlova / Baindl, Margarita Gasparyan / Betova), name orders the tiers do not read ("L. Jiajing" / "Jia Jing Lu"), and the files' own errors: a walkover the WTA's lists give as played (Sabalenka-Vondrousova, US Open 2025), the 2022 Australian Open's archived draw keeping Djokovic-Kecmanovic after Djokovic's removal, and files that disagree on an opponent (Goerges's 2020 Australian Open match against Kuzmova or Hruncakova). Within a Slam edition a namesake's match against the same opponent can be taken when the row's own is missing ('Xiyu Wang' and 'Yafan Wang'), as the generic first pass allows everywhere. Outside the Slams the generic matching still can give a match the pair's meeting at another event in the window when the row's own is missing. Flashscore and the ATP-site statistics are not read into the counts. Doubles, juniors and exhibitions are outside the map. Combining outside sources by the largest single one understates what several together would reach.
