# Lane C: paid vendors and rights holders for serve-speed data, and how to reach them (7 Oct 2026)

**Status: research, not a decision.** Nothing here is approved, and no vendor was contacted. Everything new was read on **7 October 2026**. Items carried over from earlier notes are marked with the lane and its access date:
* *(GL-C)*, *(GL-G)*, *(GL-A)*, *(GL-E)*, *(GL-F)*, *(GL-H)*: `data/studies/granular_data_leads/research_notes/` C, G, A, E, F and H (24 Sep 2026);
* *(R1)*, *(R2)*, *(R3)*: `data/studies/point_shot_sources/research_notes/` 1, 2 and 3 (23 Sep 2026);
* *(A1)*: the parallel lane `A1_tour_public.md` in this folder (7 Oct 2026).

**Question.** Which commercial and B2B vendors, and which bodies that own the raw data, can supply **first-serve speed** for the project's gaps, and how can the owner reach each one?

The gaps, in priority order:
1. ATP 250/500/1000 from May 2025;
2. WTA non-Slam, any year;
3. AO 2025, RG 2025 and RG 2026;
4. ATP non-Slam 2015–2020;
5. Challenger and WTA 125.

**What counts.**
* A per-match average first-serve speed per player is enough. Per-set or per-point speeds are fine too.
* "Fastest serve" alone is a weak substitute.
* Archive depth matters as much as the live feed.

## Method

* **Access: public pages and documentation only.**
  * I did not log in, sign up, start a trial, buy anything, submit a form or email anyone, and I decrypted nothing.
  * One provider-run Q&A surface (Stats Perform's "AgentDesk") answers questions sent as plain GET URLs. I sent 3 questions. Each answer offered to pass the question to a salesperson if an email address was supplied; none was supplied.
  * The WTA developer portal is a login-gated single-page app. Only its 492-byte shell was fetched; its JavaScript bundle was not read.
* **Tools.**
  * 61 single `curl` GETs with a Chrome user agent and no credentials (§7.1). No more than 8 requests went to any one host. Requests were sequential and several seconds apart.
  * 20 WebFetch calls and 49 web searches (§7.2 and §7.3).
  * Downloads were kept in the session scratchpad (`…/scratchpad/laneC/`), not in the repository.
* **Interpreter.** Scratch parsing (HTML to text, and reading the RapidAPI-embedded OpenAPI) used the base Miniforge interpreter, `C:\Users\zerom\miniforge3\python`, with its `pypdf 6.16.2`. `./python.sh` and the `tennis` environment were not used or changed. ## 0. Bottom line

1. **No vendor publicly documents a purchasable ATP or WTA non-Slam serve-speed archive.**
   * The raw speeds sit with the bodies that own the events: ATP via TDI, the WTA and the Slams.
   * They also sit with the firms that measure them: Hawk-Eye, SMT and, historically, FlightScope/EDH and Foxtenn.
   * Every route below is a bespoke licence, a request for permission, or a check on fill rates.
2. **Most likely to hold ATP non-Slam speed history.**
   * **TDI (Tennis Data Innovations)**, the ATP and ATP Media joint venture that "manage[s] and commercialise[s] data". ATP Tour ball tracking is now on every court [documented, R3/A1]. Sportradar's 4Sight overlays show serve speed built on "the deep data collected via TDI from all ATP events" [third-party, WF]. The ATP Stats Centre files carry per-point `serveSpeed` [documented, A1].
   * **SMT**, which "has supplier relationships with tennis' four Grand Slams and 40+ ATP/WTA Tour events" [third-party, Aug 2025]. Its serve-speed clocks date from 1989, and it states "the most complete data belongs to SportsMEDIA Technology (SMT)" [claimed, 2020].
   * **Hawk-Eye (Sony).** It acts mostly as a processor for event organisers, but says it retains data "indefinitely in order to maintain a historically accurate record" [claimed].
3. **WTA.**
   * Stats Perform holds the WTA's exclusive official data (umpire feed plus an Opta in-venue shot feed) until "the end of the decade" [claimed, GL-H]. No serve-speed field is documented, and Stats Perform's own Q&A bot "do[es]n't have enough detail … to confirm" [provider AI answer].
   * Raw WTA speeds come from SMT radar ("we've been supplying data to both tours") [claimed, 2014] and from Hawk-Eye or other ELC where installed. Live ELC is on all WTA match courts only from 2026 [documented, A1].
   * The rights holder is WTA Tour, Inc. and its commercial arm WTA Ventures.
4. **Matchstat / Tennis-API.com, verified.**
   * The RapidAPI-embedded OpenAPI documents per-match `fastestServe`, `averageFirstServeSpeed` and `averageSecondServeSpeed` in its match-statistics objects [documented].
   * **Published examples populate them only for Grand Slam matches:** US Open 2025 and 2026, plus AO and Wimbledon 2026.
   * They are `null` for Roland-Garros 2026, Indian Wells 2024–2026, Monte-Carlo 2026, the 2025 ATP Finals final and Laver Cup 2026, and `0` for a September 2026 Asian ATP event [observed].
   * The per-point `serve_speed_kmh` appears only in a marketing example the page itself calls "illustrative". The docs pages have no speed field.
5. **Sportradar.**
   * There is **no speed field** in the Tennis API v3 OpenAPI (172 KB; zero hits for speed, kmh, mph or fastest), in the Live Data tennis event list, or in the DDE statistics schema [observed].
   * Speed exists only in the DDE player-and-ball rally summary, `serve{eventId,side,speed}`, delivered as a live websocket [documented].
   * After 2026, DDE consumption moves to Live Data XML and the Unified Odds Feed, neither of which documents speed [documented].
6. **Licences block or complicate our use wherever terms could be read.**
   * **Sportradar:** prediction-market use needs written consent; there is an AI-training clause; data must be destroyed at termination [documented, re-read today].
   * **Stats Perform:** no ML training, no archive-building, no betting [documented, GL-G/H].
   * **TDI's Tennis Results site:** no text and data mining "for … training … of … statistical models or AI systems" [observed today].
   * **WTA site:** no automated harvesting [documented, GL-H].
   * Catalist (AO and ITF data) is the only distributor seen that openly permits prediction-market trading [observed]. Speed is not stated in its feed.
7. **Suggested contact order** (a suggestion, not a decision):
   1. TDI: `contact@tennisdata.com`; Head of Commercial Jonathon Thorpe.
   2. SMT: web form; Sr Business Development Manager Stephen Kemper.
   3. Hawk-Eye: `hello@hawkeyeinnovations.com`.
   4. Stats Perform and the WTA.
   5. A cheap fill-rate check of Matchstat (`api@tennis-api.com`), whose free RapidAPI tier exists, before any paid step.

## 2. Findings by vendor and body (most promising first)

### 2.1 TDI (Tennis Data Innovations), with ATP, ATP Media and the Tennis Data Platform

* **What it is** [claimed, https://www.tennisdata.com/]:
  * "TDI was established in 2020 as an independent joint venture between ATP and ATP Media, to manage and commercialise data across a variety of global markets, including betting, media and performance."
  * Headquarters: 22-24 Worple Road, Wimbledon, London, SW19 4DD. The partner logos include Hawk-Eye Innovations ("HEI LOGO"), Champion Data, Crionet and TennisViz [observed: image alt text].
* **Tennis Data Platform (TDP)** [claimed, https://www.tennisdata.com/tennis-data-platform]:
  * "In January we launched Tennis Data Platform (TDP)…"
  * It "allows TDI to ingest, merge, and deliver multiple live data sources … such as umpire scoring, player performance, and ball tracking data".
  * "It also serves as a comprehensive archive of historical data and offers an open 'sandbox' environment, encouraging third-party innovation with tennis data."
  * No sandbox URL, eligibility rule or field list is published [observed: absence].
* **Serve speed.**
  * TDI documents no field publicly [observed: absence on the TDI site]. Its free Tennis Results site has no speed [observed, GL-A].
  * Evidence that TDI holds it:
    * the ATP Stats Centre (Infosys) files carry `serveSpeed` per point and match-level first- and second-serve average speeds [documented, A1];
    * Sportradar's 4Sight overlay shows serve speed from "the deep data collected via TDI from all ATP events" (Sportcal, 21 Jun 2024) [third-party, WF];
    * ATP players can "download raw Hawkeye data" through Tennis IQ [third-party, R2: Ubitennis 2024 interview with TDI's head of product].
  * So TDI very probably holds per-serve speed for every ATP Tour match with tracking [inference, high].
* **Coverage and history.**
  * ATP Tour 2025–26: tracking on all courts [documented, R3/A1].
  * 2017–2024: Hawk-Eye at Masters 1000 and the ATP Finals, and at select 500/250 events from 2019 [search excerpt].
  * Challengers: probably sparse [inference].
  * How far back TDI's archive goes is not stated.
* **Delivery and price.** Not public. Betting and media distribution runs through Sportradar ("global data and streaming rights for betting, and media data rights, for all ATP Tour and ATP Challenger Tour events", from Dec 2023) [claimed, TDI story, https://www.tennisdata.com/stories/sportradar-launches-future-of-tennis-betting-with-atp-and-tdi].
* **Licence.**
  * The Tennis Results terms, embedded in https://results.tennisdata.com/en/faq, forbid "any text or data mining or web scraping … for any purpose, including use of our Tennis Data to offer or settle bets or the development, training, fine-tuning or validation of odds feeds, statistical models or AI systems or models" [observed today].
  * Sportradar's ATP addendum bars licensees from "unofficial TDI feeds" and from collating data [documented, R2].
  * Commercial TDI licence terms are not public.
* **Contact** [observed]:
  * `contact@tennisdata.com`. The Tennis Results terms say: "To contact us, please email contact@tennisdata.com", and "If you wish to link to or make any use of content on our Website other than that set out above, please contact contact@tennisdata.com".
  * A contact form on the homepage, https://www.tennisdata.com/ (fields: Name, Email, Subject, Message).
  * Named staff on https://www.tennisdata.com/home/people:
    * **Jonathon Thorpe**, Head of Commercial;
    * **Anthony Taylor**, Head of Product;
    * **Kelechi Amadi**, Product Manager;
    * **David Lampitt**, CEO;
    * **Rebecca Lewis**, Joint Head of Legal.
  * No phone number is published.
  * ATP Media's CEO Mark Webster is named in the Sony partnership release [observed].
* **Questions to ask.**
  1. Does the official ATP point data, or the TDP archive, include per-serve speed for ATP 250/500/1000 main draw and qualifying? This is the data behind the Stats Centre's MatchBeats and Key Stats.
  2. From which year, and at which events, for 2015–2024?
  3. Is there a per-match summary, such as average first-serve speed on first serves in plus the serve count?
  4. Is there anything for Challengers?
  5. Who is eligible for the TDP sandbox, and how?
  6. Can you supply a sample, its format and a price for a small research licence?
  7. Are model training, retention after the term, and personal prediction-market use permitted?
* **Confidence that TDI holds the data: high. Confidence that TDI will license it to an individual: low** [inference].

### 2.2 SMT (SportsMEDIA Technology) and SMT/IDS (Information and Display Systems)

* **Serve-speed measurement** [claimed, SMT press release, 25 Aug 2014, https://smt.com/smt-ids-celebrates-25-years-of-measuring-tennis-fastest-serves/]:
  * SMT/IDS "celebrates 25 years as the leading supplier of serve speed clock systems to the WTA and ATP".
  * It is "the only supplier of serve speed systems at tennis Grand Slam tournaments (the Australian Open, the French Open, Wimbledon and the US Open), starting with Wimbledon in 1989. In 1992, the SMT/IDS began supplying the clocks for the US Open."
  * "SMT/IDS' software guarantees accuracy of plus-or-minus 1 to 2 mph."
  * Rallis Pappas, then president of SMT/IDS: "The ATP and WTA tours do not consider Serve Speeds official stats, but we've been supplying data to both tours…"
  * "…the database for top 10 fastest serves in the SMT/IDS database since 1989."
* **The database** [claimed, SMT page, 26 Apr 2020, https://smt.com/who-are-the-top-10-all-time-fastest-serves-in-mens-tennis/]:
  * "The ATP, WTA and ITF don't keep official serve speed rankings for a variety reasons—serve speed isn't captured on every court at every tournament, and sometimes the technology being used isn't consistent from event to event. But the most complete data belongs to SportsMEDIA Technology (SMT), who have been tracking serve speeds with the best technology for more than 30 years."
  * The examples are mostly non-Slam ATP events: Washington 2007/2017/2019, Los Angeles 2008, Las Vegas 2006, Atlanta 2015, Beijing 2017, Rotterdam 2006, plus AO 2015 and the US Open 2004.
* **Storage and feeds** [claimed, https://www.info.smt.com/2024tenniscapabilities, "2024 Tennis Capabilities"]:
  * "Through TennisX, SMT provides a centralized hub for storing a wealth of information, spanning live scoring and statistics, historical ATP and WTA Tour data, player bios, serve speeds, player- and ball-tracking data, and even weather data."
  * The data "is efficiently distributed in real-time to … third-party data feeds".
  * Also: "Serve Speed Tracking: Monitor serve speeds with precision using TennisX's serve speed tracking capabilities".
  * SMT's EDGE/OASIS platforms and the CARAT coach app were "developed in collaboration with USTA coaches".
* **Recent deployments.**
  * **AO 2024** [third-party, Sports Video Group, 24 Jan 2024, https://www.sportsvideo.org/2024/01/24/smt-serves-up-tech-for-australian-open-tennis-australia-for-33rd-year/ (WF)]:
    * "Expanding its serve speed systems to cover all 16 courts, SMT captures serve speed, direction and player position strategy".
    * "SMT manages, supports and delivers a series of data feeds, including … point-by-point messaging for over 20 broadcaster, web, gaming/betting and player development recipients".
    * "SMT will collect and store over 2 million unique data messages."
    * Whether SMT did the same at AO 2025 was not verified.
  * **Cincinnati 2025, ATP and WTA 1000** [claimed, 7 Aug 2025, https://smt.com/smt-delivers-technology-backbone-and-production-power-for-expanded-and-refurbished-2025-cincinnati-open]:
    * "SMT is delivering the official scoring and data distribution in partnership with the ATP and WTA tours."
    * "Serve Speed Display System – Tracks and delivers real-time serve speeds to in-stadium LED boards and broadcast graphics".
  * **Scale** [third-party: Sports Business Journal text on smt.com, 11 Aug 2025, https://smt.com/smt-expands-led-display-presence-at-cincy-open/]: "SMT, which has supplier relationships with tennis' four Grand Slams and 40+ ATP/WTA Tour events…".
* **Serve speed: per serve (radar), claimed.** The format is unknown. Whether SMT keeps every serve or only maxima is not stated. Its claim to hold "the most complete data" suggests per-serve records [inference, medium].
* **Coverage and history.** Slams from 1989 and 1992; 40+ ATP and WTA events (which ones is not listed); WTA events are included [claimed]. Challengers: not stated.
* **Price and licence.** Not public. The ownership of the data (SMT, the tournament or the tour) is not stated [unknown]. The ATP and WTA "do not consider Serve Speeds official stats" [claimed 2014], which may matter for who controls the rights [inference].
* **Contact** [observed]:
  * The form at https://smt.com/contact-us/ ("General Inquiry": name, email, phone, company name, company website, message).
  * Offices listed: Durham NC, Jacksonville FL, Fremont CA, Toronto, Munich.
  * HQ: 3511 University Drive, Durham NC 27707; +1 919-493-9390 [search excerpt; not on SMT's site].
  * Named: **Stephen Kemper**, Sr. Business Development Manager, quoted on tennis in 2024 and 2025 [observed]. **Rallis Pappas**, president of SMT/IDS in 2014, may be out of date.
* **Questions to ask.**
  1. Do you keep per-serve speeds, with server, first or second serve, in or fault, and a timestamp, for the ATP and WTA events and Slams you serve?
  2. Which events and years, from 2015?
  3. Can you license the data, or must the tour or tournament authorise it, and who at the ATP, the WTA, Tennis Australia or the FFT should I ask?
  4. Do you hold AO 2025 and RG 2025–2026 speeds?
  5. What are the format, sample, price, training rights and betting use?
* **Confidence: medium–high that SMT holds non-Slam speed history for many events; low that it can license without the tours' consent** [inference].

### 2.3 Hawk-Eye Innovations (Sony)

* **What it says about data** [claimed, https://www.hawkeyeinnovations.com/data]:
  * "Hawk-Eye delivers a range of data feeds to partners to enable and support their products, including live, delayed, play-by-play and summary feeds."
  * "Hawk-Eye has a track record of collating and delivering world-class sports data for storage, aggregation, delivery & visualisation."
* **Who controls the data** [claimed, "Privacy Notice for Sports Data", last updated April 2025, https://www.hawkeyeinnovations.com/privacy-notice-for-sports-data]:
  * "Often, Hawk-Eye will be doing this as a processor (or sub-processor) of these customers. These sports customers may make use of our data to enhance their commercial products and services, including certain data feed products they may provide to third parties."
  * Hawk-Eye also acts as a controller for "combined data products used to compare athletes performances across multiple sports events".
  * "Where we collect your data in order to develop an historically accurate record of a sport or sporting event, we will retain your data indefinitely…"
* **ATP relationship.**
  * The TDI site lists Hawk-Eye as a partner [observed].
  * Sony and ATP Media signed a "multi-year strategic partnership" on 11 Nov 2025: HawkAR overlays are "powered by live data generated by Hawk-Eye's live tracking system", and HawkVISION will be on "all nine ATP Masters 1000 events and the Nitto ATP Finals in 2026" [claimed, https://www.hawkeyeinnovations.com/news/4400003/…].
* **Research precedent.** A peer-reviewed study used "data collected between 2003 and 2008 at international ATP, WTA and Grand Slam tournaments … 84 tournaments, 1729 matches, 262,596 points" of Hawk-Eye data (Rioult et al., MLSA 2015) [third-party; search excerpt plus the PDF]. The paper does not say how the data was obtained [observed: no acknowledgement found].
* **Serve speed.** Per serve: Hawk-Eye measures it wherever installed [inference, high; R3 lists Hawk-Eye feeds with speed at the Slams].
* **Coverage.** ATP Tour (all courts from 2025, where Hawk-Eye is the ELC vendor), WTA events with Hawk-Eye, and the Slams other than RG [inference from R3/A1].
* **Price and licence.** Not public. The rights belong to the event customers [claimed].
* **Contact** [observed]:
  * `hello@hawkeyeinnovations.com`;
  * +44 (0) 1256 268494 (Sony Business Europe, Jays Close, Basingstoke RG22 4SB);
  * offices in Atlanta (3625 Cumberland Blvd SE, Suite 350) and Melbourne (2-50 Elizabeth St, Kensington VIC).
  * Named: **Michael Markovich**, Chief Commercial Officer of Sony's sports businesses [observed, 2025 release].
  * The privacy contacts (`privacyoffice.seu@sony.com`; the Bird & Bird DPO) are for personal data, not licensing.
* **Questions to ask.**
  1. Do you license archived serve-speed or summary feeds directly, or only with the rights holder's consent?
  2. Which ATP and WTA events had Hawk-Eye serve speed in each year from 2015 to 2026?
  3. Is there a research-access route like the 2003–2008 dataset?
  4. Whom should I ask at TDI or the WTA?
* **Confidence that the data exists: high. That it is licensable directly: low** [inference].

### 2.4 Sportradar (Tennis API v3, Live Data, DDE, ATP Service+ and 4Sight)

* **Tennis API v3** (media API).
  * The OpenAPI at https://api.sportradar.com/tennis/trial/v3/openapi/openapi.yaml (172,376 bytes) contains **zero** occurrences of "speed", "velocity", "kmh", "km/h", "mph" or "fastest" [observed].
  * Its statistics keys run from `aces` through `volley_winners` (forehand, backhand, drop-shot, lob, overhead and return winners and errors, service points and so on). None is a speed [observed].
  * So the "Extended match stats" tier for the Slams has no speed either [observed].
* **Live Data** (bookmaker feed). The tennis event list at https://docs.sportradar.com/live-data/introduction/information-per-sport/tennis.md has scores, service faults (foot fault, net), ball position zones and so on. No speed event exists, including for "External feed (ATP, GS*)" events [observed].
* **DDE (legacy IMG ARENA), statistics stream.** The full schema has aces, double faults, 1st-serve counts and the like. **No speed** [observed, https://docs.sportradar.com/tennis/stream-endpoints-websockets/events-id-statistics-tennis/full-events-statistics-schema.md].
* **DDE player-and-ball tracking.**
  * The rally summary carries `"serve": {"eventId": …, "side": "Advantage", "speed": 98.213}` (sample created 2022-08-14) [observed in the doc example, https://docs.sportradar.com/tennis/p-and-b-tracking-websockets/rally-summary-feed.md]. Units are not stated.
  * It is delivered only as a live websocket (`wss://dde-generic-datastream.dde-prod.imgarena.dev/ws/tennis/rally/tracking/summary`) [documented].
  * "For availability of P&B Tracking feeds across specific Tournaments and Tours, please contact support@openbet.com" [documented].
* **Successor products** [documented, https://docs.sportradar.com/tennis/domain-url-changes.md]:
  * The imgarena domains are retired by 30 Sep 2026, and the "new domain url will be srarena.io".
  * "The direction of travel for consumption of data … beyond 2026 will be to utilise this via the Sportradar Live Data XML or Unified Odds Feed". Neither documents speed [observed].
  * **No archived-speed successor is documented.**
* **ATP Service+ and 4Sight.**
  * Sportradar's ATP Service+ uses "previously unavailable deep data" [claimed, TDI story].
  * 4Sight (live since March 2024): "Statistics available through 4Sight include data points such as serve speed" and "the deep data collected via TDI from all ATP events". It is "currently only available for sportsbooks" as stream overlays [third-party, Sportcal, 21 Jun 2024 (WF)].
  * Augmented Streaming shows "shot speed and speed of serve" [search excerpt].
  * Whether any raw speed reaches clients as data is **not stated**.
* **Micro-markets** for ATP (with TDI): next point outcome, total shots and last stroke type, but **no serve-speed market** was found [search excerpt].
* **Licence** [observed today, https://developer.sportradar.com/sportradar-updates/page/terms-and-conditions.md, "Last Updated: August 5, 2026"]:
  * "Customer shall not use … the Services/Rights or any related data … for any prediction market, trading platform, financial product … without Company's prior written consent".

  * §2.2 grants a licence "perpetual (solely for archiving purposes)".
