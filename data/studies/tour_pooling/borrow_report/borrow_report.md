# Tour pooling, Part A: how the tours differ in the model's inputs, and what borrowsim borrows

9 October 2026 · Tennis and market analyst, for the lead of the [tour-pooling study](../../../../docs/studies/tour_pooling/design.md) · **Inputs only: Part A reads no result.** Every table here is generated by `src/eda/tour_pooling/borrow_report.py` (full tables in [tables.md](tables.md); CSVs beside it). Part B, the segment readings, is in [../segments/segments.md](../segments/segments.md).

**Labels.** *Observed* = computed from the instance frame (`data/studies/current_best_model/pinnacle_first_2026-10-09/model_frame.parquet`) or from the similarity cache. *Code/doc* = read from a builder or a report. *Inference* = my reading.

**Which cache.** borrowsim fits from the v3 cache (`data/studies/tour_pooling/borrow_sim_v3/`): situation features, layoff days rounded to whole days. The tables here describe v3. Two earlier versions are kept as the record:
* v1 (`borrow_sim/`, every input);
* v2 (`borrow_sim_situation/`, fractional layoff days). v2's Part A outputs are in [v2_situation/](v2_situation/).

## In short

1. **The tours differ most in serving and holding.** *Observed* (tour averages, 2010–2018): men hold 76% of service games and women 64%; men hit 6.6% aces and women 3.5%; men break 24% of return games and women 36%.
2. **Some of the inputs' scale gap is tennis, and some is construction.**
   * **Construction, the 2-week form.** On the serve side, men's spread is 1.6–1.9 times women's. That is about what the builder's shrinkage alone gives: women's serve form is shrunk twice as hard (K3 = 20 against 10). *Code; the size match is inference.*
   * **Tennis, the surface IOS ratings.** These sit on a log-odds scale. Men's hold, break-point and serve-points-won ratings spread 1.1–1.4 times wider. Aces spread equally (0.98): the log-odds scale already says that an ace edge means more where aces are rarer. First serves in and double faults spread wider among women (0.77–0.92).
   * **Unstable, the matchup products.** The `prod_*` ratios (2.1–2.6) drift by season. prod_hold's ratio is 3.2 in 2016 and 1.5 in 2026.
3. **The first borrowsim draw (v1) borrowed at random.** `indoor` is missing for every women's match, so the classifier told the tours apart perfectly (AUC 1.000) and gave every match π = 0.5.
   * v2 kept only situation features, but it carried a data-clock artifact: fractional layoff days on a quarter of women's 2013–18 matches (§ 2.5).
   * v3 rounds the layoff days.
4. **The v3 draw borrows "the other tour's matches outside the Slams".**
   * Each model gives the other tour's Slam matches π = 0.000. The women's model sees no men's five-setters; the men's model sees no women's Slam matches, since best of three at a Slam never happens on the men's tour.
   * It takes the rest at about 63% on average. It leans toward 1000s and 250s, quarter-finals on and closer-priced matches, and away from grass and heavy favourites.
   * The pattern holds for the 2022, 2024 and 2026 models, in v2 and v3 alike.
   * Because the draws are coupled, about 65% of borrowsim's borrowed rows are also borrow50's. Of borrow50's rows that borrowsim lacks, 57–63% are the other tour's Slam matches.
5. **What tells the tours apart is format and calendar, not how the match looks to a bettor.** v3's AUC is 0.852–0.859. The price and rating gaps alone separate the tours weakly (AUC 0.62); format alone (best of, level, round, surface) gives 0.73.
6. **Live-data flags** (§ 3):
   * the men's 2026 US Open rows have `indoor` missing, a women-only pattern in training;
   * women's indoor could be filled from tennis-data.co.uk's `Court` column.

## 1. How the two tours differ in the model's inputs

### 1.1 The game (observed: each tour's average, Stage 4's surface averages, 2010–2018)

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Aces (share of service points) | 6.6% | 3.5% | 9.4% | 4.7% | 4.8% | 2.8% |
| Double faults | 4.1% | 5.3% | 3.7% | 4.8% | 4.0% | 5.1% |
| First serves in | 60.8% | 62.1% | 63.1% | 63.6% | 60.9% | 63.3% |
| First-serve points won | 69.8% | 62.5% | 73.6% | 65.7% | 67.5% | 60.9% |
| Second-serve points won | 49.9% | 45.0% | 51.8% | 46.6% | 49.3% | 44.8% |
| Service games held | 76.2% | 63.8% | 82.4% | 69.2% | 73.1% | 62.2% |
| Break points faced per service game | 0.58 | 0.78 | 0.47 | 0.71 | 0.64 | 0.81 |
| Return games won (breaks) | 23.8% | 36.2% | 17.6% | 30.8% | 26.9% | 37.8% |

*Inference.* The gap starts small at the point and grows at the game. Men win about 62% of service points and women 56%. Because a game amplifies the server's edge, that becomes 76% against 64% of service games held. A men's match is a series of holds decided by a few break points. A women's match has about one break in three return games, so the return game and second-serve weakness count for more.

### 1.4 What this means for the aligned arm (design doubts, for the lead)

* **The women's SD for the hold-family inputs is understated.**
  * Women's service-game counts are missing in 2013–14, so their hold ratings sit near 0 there. *Observed:* iosO_hold SD 0.02 and 0.14, against 0.36–0.40 from 2015.
  * Dividing by the 2013..Y − 1 SD (0.351) scales women's later hold values up by about 8%, and prod_hold's by more.
* **One SD per tour over 2013..Y − 1 does not hold the tours on one scale every season.**
  * Men's hold-rating spread narrowed: iosO_hold SD 0.50 in 2013–16, 0.38–0.41 in 2022–25.
  * The products widened in 2025–26.
* **For the 2-week form inputs, aligning mostly undoes the per-tour shrinkage.** That shrinkage was tuned per tour on purpose.
* **For aces, the log-odds scale already aligns.**
* *Inference:* aligned will mainly re-weight the hold and product inputs. A result either way says little about "units" in the tennis sense.

## 2. What borrowsim borrows

### 2.1 The first version borrowed at random (observed; superseded, `borrow_sim/`)

* **The defect.** `indoor` is missing on 32,507 of 32,507 women's matches and present on 34,592 of 34,719 men's.
* **The result.** With every input in the classifier, the tours separated perfectly: out-of-fold AUC 1.000 for 2019 and 2020. P(own tour) sat at its floor (≈ 1e-5) on every other-tour match, and π = 0.5000 ± 0.00002 everywhere, with effective n = n_other.
* **So that draw was borrow50 under another seed.**
  * v2 (`borrow_sim_situation/`) kept only situation inputs: the \|A − B\| of the price, the ratings, age and workload; the surface-season pairs; and surface, level, best of and round.
  * v3 (`borrow_sim_v3/`, the one borrowsim fits from) also rounds the layoff days to whole days (§ 2.5).

* **The draw is bimodal.** 22–24% of the other tour's matches are never borrowed (π < 0.05), and 20–23% always are (π = 1).
* **The dose is borrow50's.** Each model sees half of the other tour's rows in expectation, a third of the rows it trains on. The effective n is 62–63% of the other tour's matches.
* **v2 was within a few points of this** (AUC 0.866–0.878).
* **The coupled draws.** borrowsim keeps a row when u < π and borrow50 when u < 0.5, with the same u (common.py, red team S2). So a row is in both with probability min(π, 0.5): about 65% of borrowsim's borrowed rows are also borrow50's.
* **Where the two differ.** Each model differs from borrow50 by about 5,100–5,700 rows each way (2026). The other tour's Slam matches are 63% of the rows that borrow50 has and borrowsim lacks for the men's model (≈ 3,225 of 5,112), and 57% for the women's (≈ 3,221 of 5,686).

### 2.3 What tells the tours apart (observed: the same booster refitted on the 2026 model's pool with v3's features, y = women, held-out fifth)

| Feature group | AUC from the group alone | AUC without the group |
|---|---|---|
| All 17 situation features | 0.855 | – |
| Format and calendar: surface, level, best of, round | **0.733** | **0.670** |
| Surface-season pairs: matches on the surface this season, days since last match on it (whole days) | 0.613 | 0.785 |
| Price and rating gaps: \|odds\|, \|Elo\| ×2, \|points-model odds\|, \|surface offset\| | 0.616 | 0.842 |
| Age gap | 0.523 | 0.854 |
| Workload gap (sets in 14 days) | 0.521 | 0.855 |

* **Permutation importance (AUC drop, 2026, v3).** best_of 0.21, level 0.19, the larger layoff 0.07, round 0.06, the larger surface-season count 0.05, surface 0.05, the smaller layoff 0.03. Every gap is at most 0.02.
* **v2 compared.** v2's features ranked the same in 2022, 2024 and 2026. Rounding cut the surface-season pairs' own AUC from 0.658 to 0.613.
* **Best of.** A best-of-five match is a man's Slam match, and a best-of-three Slam match is a woman's.
* **Level × round.** The two tours' draw sizes differ by level, so the combination fingerprints the tour. Alone, level gives AUC 0.52 and round 0.52; with surface and best of they reach 0.73.
* **The price and rating gaps barely differ.** *Inference:* outside the format, a men's and a women's match look alike to a bettor. The favourite is as strong, and the rating gaps are a little wider among men.

* **The women's model borrows men's matches that are best of three.** It takes none of the men's five-set Slam matches. Of the rest it favours 1000s and 250s, quarter-finals on and closer-priced matches (π 0.58 in the closest third against 0.39 among heavy favourites), on hard courts more than clay and grass.
* **The men's model borrows women's matches outside the Slams.** None of the women's Slam matches is taken, since best of three at a Slam never happens for men. Of the rest it favours 250s and 1000s, quarter-finals on, closer prices (0.54 against 0.43), and clay and hard over grass, with no material tilt by season.
* **The price, in means (2026).** The men's model's borrowed matches have a mean favourite \|log-odds\| of 0.74, against 0.81 for the whole women's pool and 0.89 for its own matches. The women's model's are 0.74, against 0.89 for the men's pool and 0.81 for its own. So the draw over-corrects for women and moves away from the men's own mix.

*Inference: why the other tilts follow from the Slam exclusion.*
* Slams hold many of each tour's heaviest favourites (men's Slam median \|log-odds\| 1.08, women's 0.88, against 0.59–0.75 at 1000s, 500s and 250s).
* Wimbledon is a large share of the short grass season.
* Slam draws are mostly early rounds (R128–R32).
* So excluding the other tour's Slams makes the borrowed set lighter on heavy favourites, grass and early rounds. For grass and heavy favourites that moves it further from the own tour's mix, not closer: borrowed grass 9% against 12% in both tours.

### 2.5 The layoff artifact: found in v2, mostly removed in v3 (observed)

**The layoff values carried a data-clock fingerprint.** `surf_layoff` (days since the player's last match on the surface) is fractional on many women's matches: 24–27% in 2013–18, 16% in 2019, under 2% from 2022. For men it is 0–3% in every season. These are the match clock's estimated days for matches without a known date.
* **v2.** The classifier used the fingerprint. In the men's 2026 model, women's matches with a fractional layoff got mean π 0.26, and those without 0.57. So the men's model borrowed older women's seasons less: 0.44–0.46 against 0.56–0.57 for 2021–25. That is selection on data precision, not on tennis.
* **v3**, the lead's fix: the layoff is taken at whole days.
  * The surface-season pairs' own AUC fell from 0.658 to 0.613, and the full classifier's from 0.866 to 0.852 (2026).
  * The season tilt is mostly gone: 0.47–0.50 against 0.51–0.53.
  * A residual remains. Those 6,633 women's matches get mean π 0.42 in the men's 2026 model, against 0.52 for the rest. *Inference:* they differ in other ways (their rounded layoff values or their events); the gap is small enough to leave.

### 2.6 What borrowsim tests, compared with borrow50 (inference)

* **Same dose, coupled draws.** Both show each model half of the other tour's matches in expectation, and about 65% of their borrowed rows are shared.
* **What differs.** borrowsim takes none of the other tour's Slam matches and about 63% of the rest (2026: 15,170 of 23,890 women's non-Slam matches for the men's model; 16,264 of 26,087 men's for the women's). borrow50 takes a random half of everything.
* **So borrowsim against borrow50 mostly tests one question: "drop the other tour's Slams?"** Its secondary tilts are closer prices, later rounds and less grass. The lead declared this rule, not changed it: women never play best of five.
  * For women it removes five-set matches. Women never play them, and their favourite-longshot pattern is different: the better player wins more often over five sets.
  * For men it removes women's Slam matches. Those carry the Slam level code with best of three, a combination absent from the men's queries. They could confuse men's Slam forecasts only if the model leaned on the level more than on best of.
* **Expected effect: small.** The model already knows best of, level and the tour.

## 3. Live-data flags (inputs, observed)

* **The men's 2026 US Open (tournament date 30 Aug 2026, sealed) has `indoor` missing on all 127 rows.** Every other men's row has it. In training, a missing `indoor` means "women".
  * The pooled model also has `tour` = 0 to set it right.
  * notour, or a live men's row from an event whose indoor flag is not filled, carries a women-only signature.
  * Check where the prediction-run chain gets a men's indoor value for new events.
* **Women's indoor exists in the raw data.** tennis-data.co.uk's `Court` column covers 100% of women's matches ([inventory](../../../../docs/studies/feature_interactions/round0/inventory.md), § 0, item 4).
  * Filling it would be a data fix: it removes the structural NaN that names the tour.
  * It also lets women's indoor events carry their own flag.

## 4. Doubts about the design (for the lead)

1. **notour cannot test "no men/women information"** (agreed with the lead). `indoor`'s missingness names the tour perfectly, and the box-score scales nearly do. notour measures only the explicit flag's redundancy.
2. **borrowsim's similarity is mostly a format rule** (§ 2.4–2.6): "drop the other tour's Slams", with secondary tilts.
   * The lead declared it as such.
   * The v2 clock artifact (§ 2.5) is mostly removed in v3. A residual tilt against women's undated-match rows remains (π 0.42 against 0.52).
   * Read "borrowsim vs borrow50" on Slam and non-Slam matches separately (segments.py's `slam` cut does this).
3. **aligned's per-tour SDs are distorted** (§ 1.4) by the women's 2013–14 hold gap, the drift in hold spreads and the per-tour form shrinkage. Its reading will mix "units" with those artifacts.
4. **Favourite strength is nearly identical across tours** (the thirds' cuts coincide), so the segment "favourite strength" compares like with like across tours. Good for Part B.
