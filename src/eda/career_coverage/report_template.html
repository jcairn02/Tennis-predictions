Ten Challenger careers, checked match by match

We drew ten players at random from the ATP Challenger Tour, from the top 100 down to about No. 700. For each one we listed every professional singles match of their career from the ATP's official website, then looked for every one of those matches in the tennis data we downloaded. This page shows how much of each career we hold, what is missing and why, and how much detail we have about the matches we do hold.

Question A

Who we checked, and how they were picked

A player was eligible if they were ranked between 1 and 700 on the ATP ranking of 31 August 2026 (the newest ranking in our download), had played at least five Challenger main-draw matches in the year before, and were born in 1994 or later. The age limit keeps each whole career inside our download, which starts in 2010. We split the eligible players into five ranking bands and drew two from each band at random, so the sample runs from players on the edge of the top 100 down to players around No. 700.

Question B

Every match, lined up against the official record

The ATP's website lists every match a player has played at events that count for the ATP ranking: ITF World Tennis Tour events (the old "Futures", the lowest professional level), Challengers, and tour-level events such as ATP 250s and Grand Slams, with their qualifying rounds, plus Davis Cup. That is the official record we checked against. It covers each player's whole career, from their first professional match (2012 at the earliest) to 21 September 2026. The one kind of professional match it leaves out, qualifying at ITF events, is covered separately under C.

            Method

            How we checked

            1. The sample. Drawn by src/eda/career_coverage/select_players.py from the Tennis My Life ranking of 31 August 2026 and its Challenger results (seed 20260923). Two further players per band were drawn as reserves in case a pick's career started before 2010; none was needed.
            2. The official record. For each player and season we read the ATP website's player-activity data, and the ITF website's activity data for ITF events, on 23 September 2026. We transcribed every tournament and match line by line into text files kept with the study, and checked each season's match count against the count the site gives for each tournament. {{METHOD_ITF}}
            3. Finding each match in our data. We searched all eight sources in the download (listed below) for each player. A row counts as the same match when it is the same player, the opponent agrees, and the date falls in the same tournament week. Opponents are matched by the ATP player code where a source has it, and otherwise by name, allowing for accents, hyphens and middle names. A row that could fit two official matches equally well is counted as unmatched rather than guessed.
            4. Grading detail. Each official match takes the richest layer any source holds for it (the ladder in section D). "Serve and return statistics" means both players' serve totals are present, not just the score.

            Caveats

            • The official record is our transcription of the ATP and ITF websites through a web-reading tool. Match counts were checked per tournament, and every match our data holds was matched back to it, so an omission would show up as an unmatched row in our data. {{CAVEAT_TRANSCRIPTION}}
            • Matches outside professional events are not counted: junior events, US college (NCAA) matches, exhibitions and doubles. Several of these players played college tennis, so the official record has gaps of whole seasons that are not gaps in our data.
            • Walkovers are counted as matches because the official record lists them; there is nothing to measure in them, so none can have statistics.
            • Ten players is a small sample. It shows the pattern for this kind of player, not exact rates for the whole Challenger tour.
            • "Serve and return statistics" are the totals a scoreboard keeps (aces, double faults, serve points, first serves in, points won on first and second serve, service games, break points). They say nothing about rally length, shot types or point order unless a deeper layer exists.
